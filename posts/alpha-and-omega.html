Topical Sermons November 24, 2026

Alpha and Omega

Jesus as the Alpha and Omega means He is the only relationship that counts; when you stand before God, nothing else will matter.

  • Title refers to the first (Alpha) and last (Omega) letters of the Greek alphabet
  • Think of it as “A to Z.” Revelation 22:13 gives the sense in its own words: the First and the Last, the Beginning and the End.
  • God Himself refers to Himself as the Alpha and Omega (Revelation 1:8)
  • In Revelation 1:17–18, the risen Jesus calls himself the First and the Last. Revelation 22:13 joins those titles, and Jesus identifies himself in 22:16. I read this echo as a connection to the Trinity.

Old Testament Background

  • In Isaiah 44:6–20, God calls himself the First and the Last and contrasts himself with idols carved from wood. The risen Jesus uses that same title in Revelation 1:17–18. The echo gives the title a biblical backdrop beyond the alphabet illustration.

Big Idea

Jesus as the Alpha and Omega means He is the only relationship that counts — when you stand before God, nothing else will matter.

1

The Relationship That Counts

"'I am the Alpha and the Omega,' says the Lord God, 'who is, and who was, and who is to come, the Almighty.'"

Revelation 1:8 (NIV)
  • We can be pulled by many relationships: family, close friends, romance, marriage
  • But what does God consider the most important relationship?
  • Religious people in Matthew 7:21-23 thought they had that relationship — but Jesus said "I never knew you"
  • There's nothing more embarrassing than thinking you're in a relationship when the other person didn't get the memo
  • Questions: How is your relationship with Jesus? Would He say you spend quality time together? Do you read your Bible with the same interest you'd have in knowing someone you love?
2

A Window Into Judgment Day

"The Lord isn't really being slow about his promise, as some people think. No, he is being patient for your sake. He does not want anyone to be destroyed, but wants everyone to repent. But the day of the Lord will come as unexpectedly as a thief."

2 Peter 3:9-10 (NLT, excerpt)
  • God's motive is not to send people to hell — He wants everyone to have a chance to change
  • But the Lord will come unexpectedly, like a thief in the night
  • My first encounter in Fresno: my car was stolen. The person didn't announce, "I'm outside thieving!" It was just gone.
  • A sudden crash can remind us how quickly life changes. We don't know how long we have; the opportunity to respond is now.
  • Application: We have to realize our opportunities to follow God. Being here today is an invitation.
3

How Should We Live?

"Since everything around us is going to be destroyed like this, what holy and godly lives you should live, looking forward to the day of God and hurrying it along."

2 Peter 3:11-12 (NLT, excerpt)
  • Holy: Set apart, different, not following the crowd or society but the BIBLE
  • Godly: Aware of God's presence, thinking like Him, respecting Him, putting Him as priority
  • We're looking forward to new heavens and new earth filled with God's righteousness
  • Picture it, not a prediction (Judgment Day Good): Imagine waking up younger, being led to an incredible palace, God walking down from His throne to meet you, hugging you with warmth like a thousand arms
  • Picture it, not a prediction (Judgment Day Bad): Cold surroundings, God searching for your name in a dusty book, that somber voice saying "Away from me, I never knew you"
  • At that moment, nothing else matters — school, degree, job, "finding the one," financial stability — just utter regret for living selfishly

Conclusion

  • Summary: He is the Alpha and the Omega — the relationship that counts above all else
  • Call to Action: Make every effort to figure out if you're in the relationship God desires. Ask to study the Bible.
  • Closing: Jesus is the beginning and the end. Make sure you know Him before the end comes.
