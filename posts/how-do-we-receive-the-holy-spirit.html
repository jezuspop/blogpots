How Do We Receive the Holy Spirit?

What does it mean for the Holy Spirit to be poured out, to dwell in us, and to be received through the laying on of hands?

  • The study of the Holy Spirit has many parts:
    • Who or what is the Holy Spirit? (The role within the Trinity)
    • What does the Spirit do? (Actions through NT examples)
    • How do you get the Holy Spirit? (Various forms of receiving)
  • This study covers the last question — directly addressing misconceptions from charismatic groups
  • Prerequisite: A solid understanding of biblical baptism

Big Idea

The scriptures point to the indwelling of the Holy Spirit through water baptism as the standard for salvation — not the dramatic outpouring or laying of hands that some groups emphasize.

1

Outpouring — God's Spirit Poured Out

"When the day of Pentecost came, they were all together in one place. Suddenly a sound like the blowing of a violent wind came from heaven and filled the whole house where they were sitting. They saw what seemed to be tongues of fire that separated and came to rest on each of them. All of them were filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak in other tongues as the Spirit enabled them."

Acts 2:1-4 (NIV)
  • What is it? God's Spirit pouring onto people (prophesied in Isaiah 44:3, Zechariah 12:10, Joel 2:28-29)
  • What's its purpose? God's way of highlighting significant events
  • Only happens twice in Acts:
    • The beginning of the church (Acts 2:1-4)
    • The acceptance of the Gentiles (Acts 10:44-45)
  • How is it received? Pouring over/immersing groups of people — charismatic groups call this "Baptism of the Holy Spirit"
  • This was exceptional, not normative
2

Indwelling — God's Spirit Dwelling in Us

"Peter replied, 'Repent and be baptized, every one of you, in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins. And you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. The promise is for you and your children and for all who are far off—for all whom the Lord our God will call.'"

Acts 2:38-39 (NIV)

What is it?
God's Spirit dwelling in us (Ezekiel 36:24-27, 1 Cor 3:16, 1 Cor 6:19, Romans 8:9, Galatians 4:6)

What's its purpose?
Salvation, forgiveness of sins, indwelling of Holy Spirit

How is it received?
Through water baptism (Acts 2:38, Acts 8:36-38, Acts 10:47)

This is the standard
— the only explicit command about receiving the Holy Spirit

3

Transferring — The Laying of Hands

"When the apostles in Jerusalem heard that Samaria had accepted the word of God, they sent Peter and John to Samaria. When they arrived, they prayed for the new believers there that they might receive the Holy Spirit, because the Holy Spirit had not yet come on any of them; they had simply been baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus. Then Peter and John placed their hands on them, and they received the Holy Spirit."

Acts 8:14-17 (NIV)
  • What is it? Holy Spirit being passed to one another, leading to miraculous gifts
  • What’s its purpose? The signs in Acts confirm the message (Acts 14:3; Hebrews 2:3–4). Mark 16:15–18 belongs to the longer ending, absent from some early manuscripts. I read the signs as descriptions, not instructions for believers to attempt.
  • How is it received? The laying of hands by apostles (Acts 6:3-6, Acts 8:14-19, Acts 19:5-6, Romans 1:11-12)
  • Acts 8 and 19 describe these events after baptism.
  • Exception: Not all laying of hands transferred the Spirit — Jesus blessing children, healing the sick, commissioning for ministry
4

The Indwelling Is the Standard

  • Acts 2:38 is the only explicit command about receiving the Holy Spirit — other examples are just examples
  • There can only be one baptism (Ephesians 4:4-6) — the indwelling through water baptism
  • Apostles were not present at every baptism (Acts 8:36-39 — the Ethiopian Eunuch)
    • Acts does not describe an apostle participating in his baptism; it says the man went on his way rejoicing.
    • Without God's Spirit, you don't belong to Christ (Romans 8:9-10)
    • Either he wasn't saved, or he received the indwelling through baptism
  • The outpouring appears too few times to consider it the normal way to receive the Spirit
  • The miraculous gifts would come to an end (1 Corinthians 13:8-10)
    • Church fathers Origen, Augustine, and Chrysostom all noted the cessation of miraculous gifts

Conclusion

  • Summary: The scriptures point to receiving the indwelling as the norm for salvation through water baptism
  • Call to Action: Don't confuse these distinct forms of receiving the Spirit. Hold to what Scripture explicitly commands.
  • Closing: Pray expectantly as James 5:13–18 teaches. My reading is that the apostolic signs served a particular purpose in establishing the message.
