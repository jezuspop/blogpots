Topical Sermons November 24, 2026

The Anointed One

A woman gives Jesus something costly, and He says she is preparing Him for burial.

  • Theme: Devoted to Understanding—deeper understanding leads to inspiration and hope
  • Today we go Micro: breaking down one passage to uncover awesome inspiration
  • When we take time for deeper understanding, the Bible reveals hidden layers and insight

Big Idea

A woman gives Jesus something costly, and He says she is preparing Him for burial. I also see a picture of honoring the true King. What fragrance does our devotion leave behind?

1

The Expensive Perfume

"A woman came to him with an alabaster jar of very expensive perfume, which she poured on his head as he was reclining at the table."

Matthew 26:7 (NIV)
  • Matthew 26 and Mark 14 describe closely parallel scenes. John 12 has its own details, and Luke 7 tells an anointing story in a different setting.
  • Mark says the perfume could have sold for more than three hundred denarii, a costly act of devotion. You can see why people reacted so strongly.
  • The woman’s action also brings to mind the Old Testament’s anointing of kings.
2

The Anointing of Kings

"Zadok the priest took the horn of oil from the sacred tent and anointed Solomon. Then they sounded the trumpet and all the people shouted, 'Long live King Solomon!'"

1 Kings 1:39 (NIV)
  • Anointing could mark someone as king, as it does for Solomon in 1 Kings 1:39. That image helps me see the woman’s gift as royal honor. Matthew doesn’t say that was her intent; Jesus himself tells us it prepared Him for burial.
  • Matthew also records the Magi bringing Jesus gifts of gold, frankincense, and myrrh (Matthew 2:11).

Questions:

  • How quick are you to anoint Jesus King of your life?
  • Anoint Him with your time? Your attention? Your finances? Your heart?

Conviction: Sometimes I feel like I douse Jesus with cheap Axe Body Spray! I can easily see myself responding like the disciples.

3

The Passion Week Fragrance

"When she poured this perfume on my body, she did it to prepare me for burial."

Matthew 26:12 (NIV)

"Who is this coming up from the wilderness like a column of smoke, perfumed with myrrh and incense made from all the spices of the merchant?"

Song of Songs 3:6 (NIV)
  • A fragrance like that would have filled the room. I like to imagine the scent lingering into the Passion as a reminder of who Jesus was. The Gospels don’t say it lingered; the scene gives us the image, and Jesus gives us its connection to His burial.

The Application: We Carry the Perfume of Royalty

"But thank God! He has made us his captives and continues to lead us along in Christ's triumphal procession. Now he uses us to spread the knowledge of Christ everywhere, like a sweet perfume. Our lives are a Christ-like fragrance rising up to God. But this fragrance is perceived differently by those who are being saved and by those who are perishing."

2 Corinthians 2:14-15 (NLT)
  • Paul picked up on this connection—we now carry the perfume of royalty through Jesus
  • We carry it by living our lives according to the Bible and studying with others
  • What kind of perfume are you carrying?
    • Is it the cheap imitation that smells nothing like the truth?
    • Or the life-giving perfume of Christ that changes people's lives forever?

Personal Conviction: My perfume of Christ in my household when it comes to devotionals or praying with my children can be the cheap imitation. I need to give more time, my best in those moments.

Conclusion

  • Summary: The woman honored Jesus with a costly gift. Jesus connected it to His burial, and I hear an invitation to honor Him as our King.
  • Call to Action: Get someone in your life who will help you smell more like Christ
  • Closing: Let's decide today to live lives that give off the fragrance of our Anointed King, Jesus
