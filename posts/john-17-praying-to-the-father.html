John 17: Jesus Prays the Mission Home

Read Jesus’ prayer in its Farewell Discourse setting, following its themes of glory, mission, unity, and presence with the Father.

Introduction & Context

John 17 is the climax of the Farewell Discourse (John 13-17). After washing the disciples' feet (John 13), comforting their troubled hearts (John 14), calling them to abide (John 15), and explaining the Spirit's coming advantage (John 16), Jesus now turns from teaching the disciples to praying to the Father.

The chapter is often called the High Priestly Prayer, though John never uses that title. The title is fitting because Jesus intercedes for his people on the eve of sacrifice, but the chapter is also royal, filial, missional, and deeply relational. Jesus prays as the Son returning to the Father's glory, the sent one completing the Father's work, and the shepherd guarding the people the Father gave him.

The timing matters. Jesus is hours from betrayal, arrest, abandonment, trial, torture, and crucifixion. Yet his prayer is not panic. It is ordered, clear, intimate, and mission-focused.

Big Idea

On the night before the cross, Jesus prays that the Father would glorify the Son through the completion of his mission, protect and sanctify his disciples, and unite future believers so the world may see the Father's love in the Son's people.

Driving Question

What does Jesus want most for his people when he prays on the edge of the cross?

Simple Movement

  1. Glorify the Son -- John 17:1-5
  2. Keep and sanctify the disciples -- John 17:6-19
  3. Unite the believers for witness and love -- John 17:20-26

Key Themes

  1. Glory through the cross -- John 17:1, 4-5
    • In John, glory is not escape from suffering but revelation through suffering.
  2. Eternal life as relational knowing -- John 17:3
    • Eternal life is not merely endless duration; it is knowing the Father and the Son.
  3. The Father's gift to the Son -- John 17:2, 6, 9, 24
    • The disciples belong to the Father and are given to Jesus.
  4. The revelation of the Father's name -- John 17:6, 11-12, 26
    • Jesus makes God's character, authority, and covenant identity known.
  5. Protection, not escape -- John 17:11, 15
    • Jesus does not ask that disciples be removed from the world but kept from the evil one.
  6. Joy inside hostile mission -- John 17:13-14
    • Jesus prays for joy even while acknowledging the world's hatred.
  7. Sanctification by truth -- John 17:17, 19
    • The disciples are set apart by the Father's word and by Jesus' self-consecration.
  8. Sent as Jesus was sent -- John 17:18
    • Mission is patterned after Jesus' own sending.
  9. Unity for witness -- John 17:20-23
    • The unity of believers is tied to the world's recognition of Jesus' mission.
  10. Love before the foundation of the world -- John 17:24, 26
  • The love between Father and Son becomes the source and destiny of the church.

Passage Study

Introduction

John 17 lets us overhear the prayer life of Jesus at the most important moment of his earthly ministry. The cross is not an accident Jesus is trying to survive. It is the hour through which the Son will glorify the Father and the Father will glorify the Son. Before Jesus is arrested, he prays the meaning of the cross over himself and over his people.

This chapter is not mainly a model prayer for us, though it teaches us how to pray. It is first the prayer Jesus prays for us. He prays for our protection, holiness, mission, unity, joy, and final presence with him.

One-sentence sermon summary:

Jesus prays that his completed mission would glorify the Father, secure his disciples in the world, and unite future believers as visible witnesses of divine love.

1

Jesus Prays for Glory Through Completed Mission (John 17:1-5)

Read the passage: John 17:1

Main Point: Jesus does not ask to avoid the hour. He asks that the hour reveal glory. The cross is not a detour around glory; in John, the cross is the path of glory. Jesus has completed the work the Father gave him, and now he asks to be glorified with the preexistent glory he had with the Father before the world existed.

Exegetical Notes

  • "The hour has come" (John 17:1) recalls the repeated Johannine theme that Jesus' hour had not yet come (John 2:4; 7:30; 8:20) until Passion week (John 12:23, 27; 13:1).
  • "Glorify" translates δοξάζω (doxazō). In John, glory is revealed most fully in the cross, resurrection, and return to the Father.
  • "Eternal life" (John 17:3) is defined relationally: knowing the only true God and Jesus Christ whom he sent.
  • "I glorified you on earth" (John 17:4) means Jesus' entire incarnate mission revealed the Father.
  • "Before the world existed" (John 17:5) points back to the preexistent Word in John 1:1-2.

Theological Weight

John 17:3 does not reduce eternal life to information. "Knowing" in biblical language is relational, covenantal, and participatory. Eternal life is fellowship with the Father through the Son.

Best Teaching Line

Jesus defines eternal life not first as a place you go, but as a Person you know.

Application

Some disciples want God to remove hard moments before they reveal anything. Jesus shows a different path: he asks the Father to be glorified through the hour, not by avoiding it.

Takeaway

The cross is not Jesus losing glory. The cross is where divine glory becomes visible in sacrificial love.

2

Jesus Prays for His Disciples in the World (John 17:6-19)

Read the passage: John 17:11

Main Point: Jesus does not pray for the disciples to be removed from danger. He prays that the Father would keep them, unite them, fill them with joy, protect them from the evil one, sanctify them in truth, and send them into the world.

Exegetical Notes

  • "I have manifested your name" (John 17:6) means Jesus has revealed the Father's character, authority, identity, and covenant presence.
  • "They were yours, and you gave them to me" (John 17:6) emphasizes divine ownership before discipleship.
  • "Keep them in your name" (John 17:11) is protection language rooted in God's revealed identity.
  • "That they may be one" (John 17:11) begins the unity theme that expands in John 17:20-23.
  • "I do not ask that you take them out of the world" (John 17:15) destroys escapist discipleship.
  • "Sanctify them in the truth; your word is truth" (John 17:17) frames holiness as consecration for mission through God's revelation.
  • "As you sent me... so I have sent them" (John 17:18) gives the disciples a mission patterned after Jesus' own sending.

Key Tension

Jesus prays:

  • In the world, but not of the world
  • Protected, but not removed
  • Holy, but not isolated
  • Sent, but not self-powered

Best Teaching Line

Jesus does not pray for a safe church. He prays for a sanctified church sent into an unsafe world.

Application

Many disciples want holiness through withdrawal. Jesus prays for holiness inside mission. The church is not preserved by hiding from the world but by being kept by the Father, sanctified by truth, and sent like Jesus.

Takeaway

The church is not a bunker. It is a sanctified people sent into enemy territory with the Father's name over them.

3

Jesus Prays for Future Believers and Visible Unity (John 17:20-23)

Read the passage: John 17:20

Main Point: Jesus prays beyond the room. He sees future believers who will come through apostolic witness, and he prays for their unity as a visible sign to the world that the Father sent the Son.

Exegetical Notes

  • "Those who will believe in me through their word" (John 17:20) includes every future believer reached through apostolic testimony.
  • "That they may all be one" (John 17:21) is not vague friendliness. It is unity grounded in the Father-Son relationship.
  • "So that the world may believe" (John 17:21) ties unity to mission.
  • "The glory that you have given me I have given to them" (John 17:22) means believers participate in the Son's mission-shaped glory.
  • "That they may become perfectly one" (John 17:23) uses completion/perfection language, not superficial harmony.
  • "That the world may know" (John 17:23) repeats the witness function of unity.

Theological Weight

John 17 unity is not organizational sameness, personality compatibility, or conflict avoidance. It is shared life in the Father and Son that becomes visible through love, truth, holiness, and mission.

Best Teaching Line

Unity is not the mission, but without unity the mission loses credibility.

Application

Disunity does not merely hurt feelings. It distorts the witness of the church. Jesus prays that the world would see something in the unity of believers that points back to the sending love of the Father.

Takeaway

The world has the right to inspect our love when we claim to represent Jesus.

4

Jesus Prays for Final Presence and Shared Love (John 17:24-26)

Read the passage: John 17:24

Main Point: Jesus' final desire is presence. He wants his people with him to behold his glory and to live inside the love the Father has for the Son.

Exegetical Notes

  • "I desire" (John 17:24) is strikingly personal. Jesus expresses holy desire to the Father.
  • "Whom you have given me" repeats the gift-language of the chapter.
  • "May be with me where I am" is the relational goal of salvation.
  • "To see my glory" means final participation in the revelation of the Son.
  • "You loved me before the foundation of the world" (John 17:24) reaches back into eternal Father-Son love.
  • "I made known to them your name, and I will continue to make it known" (John 17:26) shows Jesus' revelation continues through the Spirit and the church's life.
  • "That the love with which you have loved me may be in them, and I in them" (John 17:26) is one of the deepest union-with-Christ statements in John.

Best Teaching Line

The end of salvation is not merely that we escape judgment. The end is that we are brought into the love the Father has for the Son.

Application

Jesus does not merely want useful servants. He wants his people with him. Mission is real, holiness is real, unity is real, but the final horizon is presence with Christ and participation in divine love.

Takeaway

Jesus' final prayer is not that we would do more for him, but that we would be with him and know the Father's love in him.

Takeaway

  • Jesus prays before the cross because the cross is not chaos to him.
  • Eternal life is not just living forever; it is knowing the Father through the Son.
  • Jesus does not pray us out of the world; he prays us into mission.
  • Truth sanctifies; sentiment does not.
  • Unity is not optional because witness is not optional.
  • The world should be able to look at the church's love and see evidence that the Father sent the Son.
  • Jesus' final desire is presence: "that they may be with me where I am."

Reflection Questions

  1. Do I define eternal life mainly as heaven later or knowing God now?
  2. Am I asking God to remove me from the world when Jesus prayed to send me into it?
  3. Is my holiness connected to mission or just personal avoidance?
  4. Where am I resisting sanctification by truth?
  5. Does my relationship with other believers make Jesus' mission more believable or less believable?
  6. Am I protecting unity without truth, or truth without love?
  7. Do I believe Jesus actually desires me to be with him?
  8. What would change if I saw John 17 as Jesus praying over me?

Connection to the Gospel

John 17 shows that salvation is bigger than forgiveness alone. Jesus prays us into the life of God: forgiven, guarded, sanctified, sent, united, loved, and brought home to behold his glory.

The cross answers Jesus' prayer. Through the cross, the Son glorifies the Father. Through the resurrection and ascension, the Father glorifies the Son. Through the Spirit and apostolic witness, disciples are kept, sanctified, sent, and united. The prayer of John 17 becomes the shape of the church's life.

Putting the Study into Practice

  • Receive eternal life as knowing God through Jesus.
  • Let the Father's name define your identity.
  • Stay in the world without belonging to the world.
  • Be sanctified by truth.
  • Guard unity because mission is at stake.
  • Live as someone Jesus prayed for before the cross.
  • Move toward the final desire of Jesus: being with him and beholding his glory.
