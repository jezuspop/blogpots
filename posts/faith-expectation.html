Topical Sermons November 24, 2026

Faith Expectation

Faith gives us the confidence to expect God to be faithful. James asks us to show that confidence in what we do.

  • This month's focus: Faith. A common way of talking about faith is believing what you want to believe even when you can't prove it. That's not what I'm talking about today.
  • Faith doesn't make God real. Faith responds to a real God who wants to be known.
  • If I trust someone, I expect them to be reliable. What would I do differently if I really expected God to be faithful?

Big Idea

Faith gives us the confidence to expect God to be faithful. James asks us to show that confidence in what we do.

1

Faith Is Expectation

Hebrews 11:1

What I mean by expectation

  • Hebrews describes being sure of what we hope for and certain of what we do not see.
  • Preaching emphasis: Because I trust God, I expect him to be true and reliable. That confidence rests on who God is, even when I can't see what's ahead.
2

Faith Demands Action

James 2:14, 17

What good is it?

  • James gives us an example: a brother or sister needs clothes and food. What good are warm words if we leave them cold and hungry (vv. 15–16)?
  • In v. 18 the challenge is to show faith by what we do. A claim to faith without deeds is what James calls dead.
  • We believe in devotion to God, fruitfulness, discipling one another, seeing more people become Christians, righteousness, repentance, and family devos.
  • Question: Where is there a gap between what we say we believe and what we've actually done?
3

God Equips Moses for the Work

Read Exodus 3:10–12; 4:2–17

  • At the burning bush, God sends Moses to Pharaoh. When Moses asks who he is to do it, God promises to be with him (Exodus 3:10–12).
  • God gives Moses words to bring to Israel, signs to confirm the message, including the staff, and Aaron to speak with him (Exodus 3:14–22; 4:2–8, 14–17).
  • Moses hesitates. God doesn't leave him with a bare announcement that Israel needs leaders. He sends him and supplies help for the work.
  • Application: Moses could act because God was with him. Our expectations are grounded in the God who calls and equips us.

What Obedience Cost in Egypt

  • Moses was sent into a system of forced labor. After his first appeal, Pharaoh made the Israelites gather their own straw without reducing their brick quota. Their conditions got worse before they got better. That part of Moses’ story keeps us from measuring faith by how quickly things improve. Exodus 3:7–12 · Exodus 5:6–19

Conclusion

  • Let your actions reveal your faith. What step can you take this week that shows you trust what God says?
