Increase Our Faith: Struggle Increases Faith

Trials purify and increase our faith like fire refines gold; when we struggle for our relationship with God, against sin, and together with God's…

  • Series: Increase Our Faith
  • Theme: By Faith
  • How struggling through problems and opposition increases our faith
  • My challenge: Get BETTER at struggling

Big Idea

Trials purify and increase our faith like fire refines gold—when we struggle for our relationship with God, against sin, and together with God's people, our faith grows genuine and strong.

1

Trials Refine Our Faith (1 Peter 1:3–9)

"So be truly glad. There is wonderful joy ahead, even though you must endure many trials for a little while. These trials will show that your faith is genuine. It is being tested as fire tests and purifies gold—though your faith is far more precious than mere gold. So when your faith remains strong through many trials, it will bring you much praise and glory and honor on the day when Jesus Christ is revealed to the whole world."

1 Peter 1:6-7 (NLT)
  • While on earth, we will have trials of many kinds
  • These trials show our faith is genuine—tested like gold refined
  • Stay with Peter’s picture: fire tests gold. Trials can reveal and strengthen a faith more precious than gold.
2

Struggling for Our Relationship with God (Genesis 32:22–32)

"This left Jacob all alone in the camp, and a man came and wrestled with him until the dawn began to break... 'I will not let you go unless you bless me.'... 'Your name will no longer be Jacob,' the man told him. 'From now on you will be called Israel, because you have fought with God and with men and have won.'"

Genesis 32:24–28 (NLT, selected excerpts)

The Night Before Esau

  • Jacob hears that Esau is coming with 400 men. Afraid, he prays, sends gifts ahead, and moves his family across the Jabbok. The wrestling happens in the middle of that crisis. At dawn Jacob has a blessing and a limp. Genesis 32

Determined to Fight (vv. 23–24)

  • Jacob sent his family and possessions across the stream and was left alone.
  • The man wrestled with him until daybreak. Think about giving God that kind of undivided attention.

Giving Your Best (v. 25)

  • The man touched Jacob’s hip and wrenched it. Jacob kept holding on.
  • When was the last time you prayed with your whole heart? The point isn’t to injure yourself or get loud. It’s to bring God more than a halfhearted prayer.
  • E. M. Bounds makes the same appeal in The Weapon of Prayer, ch. 1: don’t treat prayer as secondary; let it engage you fully.

Not Stopping Until You See Change (vv. 26–29)

  • Jacob refused to let go without a blessing (v. 26); he was blessed in v. 29.

Given a New Name (v. 28)

  • The story connects the name Israel with struggling with God and people and prevailing (v. 28). Jacob receives the name as an honor.
  • I want that title

Challenge: Go find your Peniel and make it your place to wrestle with God

3

Struggling Against the Sin of This World (Ephesians 6:10–18)

"For our struggle is not against flesh and blood, but against the rulers, against the authorities, against the powers of this dark world and against the spiritual forces of evil in the heavenly realms."

Ephesians 6:12 (NIV)
  • Paul pictures close combat, but explicitly says the enemy is not flesh and blood.
  • I can feel unprepared or surprised by sin
  • I can't believe they responded that way! What should I expect? I'M IN A WAR!
  • No one goes to war and thinks, "Wow, I can't believe he shot at me—how rude!"
  • The world is not going to warn you or apologize for attacking you

Question: How many of us are truly struggling with sin the way the Bible describes?

Challenge: Picture the spiritual battlefield when you struggle with the world

4

Struggling Together As God's People (Romans 15:30; Hebrews 3:12–14)

"I urge you, brothers, by our Lord Jesus Christ and by the love of the Spirit, to join me in my struggle by praying to God for me."

Romans 15:30 (NIV)

"Encourage one another daily, as long as it is called Today, so that none of you may be hardened by sin's deceitfulness."

Hebrews 3:13 (NIV)

What Paul Asked Them to Pray For

  • Paul was headed to Jerusalem with an offering from believers in Macedonia and Achaia. He asked the Christians in Rome to pray for his safety in Judea and for the gift to be welcomed. They could take part in work happening far from home by praying with him. Romans 15:25–33
  • Paul wanted the church to strive TOGETHER with him in his struggles
  • This bonded the church to Jesus through Paul's struggles
  • Personal: Some of the friendships that fuel my devotion to Jesus started on runs, working out, camping, or praying together. Those are my struggle buddies.

Questions

  • Do you join the struggle with others?
  • Are you helping those around you who are hurting?

Challenge: Identify your "Struggle Buddies" and spend time with them consistently

Conclusion

  • Summary: Get better at struggling in three areas—relationship with God, against sin, and together with God's people
  • Call to Action: Find your Peniel, picture the battlefield, identify your Struggle Buddies
  • Closing: In so doing, we will increase our faith like Peter, whose words still call the church to endure. Early Christian tradition remembers his faithful death (Eusebius, Church History 2.25).

Brief Study Notes

  • Genesis 32:24–29; 1 Peter 1:6–7: Jacob’s excerpt is NLT. Keep the gold image tied to Peter’s testing and refining metaphor.
