Topical Sermons November 24, 2026

Getting Back to Struggling

Struggling is truly fighting with all you have; it's crucial for spiritual survival and growth. Get back to struggling with God, together, and against sin.

  • Theme: Fight. We’re entering the 11th month with a focus on gratitude. This is a check-up: How is the fight going?
  • We live in a world where people don’t want to struggle, or even be seen struggling. Picture a butterfly emerging from its chrysalis. Not all effort means something has gone wrong.

Big Idea

Struggling is truly fighting with all you have — it's crucial for spiritual survival and growth. Get back to struggling with God, together, and against sin.

1

Struggling with God (Genesis 32:22–32)

"Then the man said, 'Your name will no longer be Jacob, but Israel, because you have struggled with God and with men and have overcome.'"

Genesis 32:28 (NIV)

The Night Before Esau

  • Jacob hears that Esau is coming with 400 men. Afraid, he prays, sends gifts ahead, and moves his family across the Jabbok. The wrestling happens in the middle of that crisis. At dawn Jacob has a blessing and a limp. Genesis 32
  • "Struggle" (Hebrew) means to contend or contest with
  • Jacob's example:
    • Sent his family and possessions across the stream and was left alone (vv. 23–24). Picture giving God your undivided attention.
    • Wrestled through the night. The man touched Jacob’s hip and wrenched it (vv. 24–25).
    • Would not let go without a blessing (v. 26). The blessing comes in v. 29.
  • A new name (v. 28): The story links Israel with struggling with God and people and prevailing. Jacob receives it as a blessing, not a reason for shame.
  • Application: Do we take our life situations to God ready to struggle with all our effort and might?
  • Like the persistent widow (Luke 18:1–8), keep praying and don’t give up.
2

Struggling Together (Romans 15:30; Hebrews 3:12–14)

"I urge you, brothers, by our Lord Jesus Christ and by the love of the Spirit, to join me in my struggle by praying to God for me."

Romans 15:30 (NIV)

What Paul Asked Them to Pray For

  • Paul was headed to Jerusalem with an offering from believers in Macedonia and Achaia. He asked the Christians in Rome to pray for his safety in Judea and for the gift to be welcomed. They could take part in work happening far from home by praying with him. Romans 15:25–33
  • Paul asks the church to strive alongside him. In this case, the way they join his struggle is by praying.
  • Paul makes his appeal by the Lord Jesus Christ and the love of the Spirit.
  • This bonded the church to Jesus through Paul's struggles
  • Some of my closest relationships grew out of showing up for one another through early-morning prayer, fasting, and difficult seasons. Those friendships fuel my devotion to Jesus.
  • Application: Do you join the struggle with others? Are you helping those who are hurting?
  • Hebrews 3:12-14: Encourage one another daily so none may be hardened by sin's deceitfulness
3

Struggling with Sin (Ephesians 6:10–18)

"For our struggle is not against flesh and blood, but against the rulers, against the authorities, against the powers of this dark world and against the spiritual forces of evil in the heavenly realms."

Ephesians 6:12 (NIV)
  • Paul uses the image of a wrestling match for the spiritual conflict. People are not the enemy.
  • Paul brings to light the reality of our struggle — it cannot be taken lightly
  • The wrong use of "struggling": I would confess "struggling with anger" but there was no battling through emotions to see change. I simply gave in.
  • Are you truly struggling with:
    • Pressures at school to follow the crowd
    • Pride at home to rebel against parents
    • Anger that separates your marriage and parenting
    • Bad behavior in the workplace where convictions are tested
    • Fear to share your faith
    • Fear to study the Bible and trust God
  • Jesus' example (Hebrews 5:7-10): He offered prayers and petitions with loud cries and tears — Hebrews calls Him the source of eternal salvation for those who obey Him.

Conclusion

  • Summary: Get back to struggling — with God, together, and against sin
  • Call to Action: Stop using "struggling" incorrectly. Actually fight with all you have.
  • Closing: What will your struggle lead you to? Jesus' struggle led to eternal life for us.

Brief Study Notes

  • Genesis 32:22–32; Romans 15:30; Ephesians 6:10–18; Hebrews 5:7–10: The hip injury, communal prayer, spiritual opposition, and Jesus’ obedience belong to the passages. The butterfly is only an illustration.
