John 1:17: Moses, Torah, Grace, and Truth

Read the language of John 1:17 alongside Exodus and John’s Gospel to see how Torah, grace, and truth fit together.

Interpretive balance added in review

The absence of an explicit Greek “but” does not exclude a contrast. Asyndeton can carry a contrast inferred from context, and translators who supply a connective need not be distorting the Greek. Distinguish an opposition between good and bad revelation from a contrast between successive stages of gracious revelation.

John 1:16, charin anti charitos, is also debated: overflowing grace and grace replacing earlier grace are both important readings. The latter can understand the Mosaic gift as genuinely gracious while affirming its surpassing fulfillment in Christ. The continuity-and-fulfillment reading emphasized below is defensible, but should not be presented as the only reading grammar permits. “Grace and truth” likely evokes Exodus 34:6 thematically; it is not an exact reproduction of the Septuagint’s wording there.

Study question: What does the text explicitly say, what relationship does the translator infer, and what does the whole Prologue contribute?

Commentary Notes

The Question

The issue:

Is it true that in John 1:17 there should not be a “but” separating Moses and Torah from Jesus, grace, and truth? Is “but” inserted in English translation?

Short Answer

Yes, the Greek text of John 1:17 does not contain an explicit Greek word for “but” between the two clauses.

The verse is asyndetic: the two clauses are placed side by side without a connective such as ἀλλά (alla, “but”), δέ (de, “but/and/now”), or καί (kai, “and”).

So translations that include “but” are making an interpretive decision, not translating an explicit Greek conjunction.

That does not automatically mean every translation with “but” is malicious or heretical. Translators sometimes add English connectives to clarify what they believe the relationship between clauses is. But in this verse, “but” can overstate contrast and imply that John is setting Moses/Torah against Jesus/grace/truth in a way the Greek wording itself does not require.

Big Idea

John 1:17 does not present Moses and Jesus as enemies. It presents Torah through Moses as God’s real gift and Jesus Christ as the climactic embodiment of God’s covenant grace and truth.

Driving Question

Is John contrasting Moses and Jesus, or is he showing that God’s revelation through Moses reaches its fullness in Jesus Christ?

The Greek Text

John 1:17 Greek

ὅτι ὁ νόμος διὰ Μωϋσέως ἐδόθη, ἡ χάρις καὶ ἡ ἀλήθεια διὰ Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ ἐγένετο.

Transliteration

hoti ho nomos dia Mouseos edothe, he charis kai he aletheia dia Iesou Christou egeneto.

Wooden Translation

For the law through Moses was given; the grace and the truth through Jesus Christ came / came to be.

Clean Translation

For the Law was given through Moses; grace and truth came through Jesus Christ.

Key Observation

There is no Greek word between:

  • ὁ νόμος διὰ Μωϋσέως ἐδόθη – “the law was given through Moses”
  • ἡ χάρις καὶ ἡ ἀλήθεια διὰ Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ ἐγένετο – “grace and truth came through Jesus Christ”

The relationship between the clauses has to be inferred from context, not from an explicit conjunction.

Translation Comparison

Translation Rendering Effect
KJV “For the law was given by Moses, but grace and truth came by Jesus Christ.” Strong contrast.
NKJV “For the law was given through Moses, but grace and truth came through Jesus Christ.” Strong contrast.
NET “For the law was given through Moses, but grace and truth came about through Jesus Christ.” Strong contrast; NET note explicitly reads old/new covenant contrast.
ESV “For the law was given through Moses; grace and truth came through Jesus Christ.” Juxtaposition without “but.”
NIV “For the law was given through Moses; grace and truth came through Jesus Christ.” Juxtaposition without “but.”
NASB 1995 “For the Law was given through Moses; grace and truth were realized through Jesus Christ.” Juxtaposition; “realized” interprets fulfillment.
NRSV “The law indeed was given through Moses; grace and truth came through Jesus Christ.” Mild concession/continuity; no stark “but.”

Best Working Rendering

The Law was given through Moses; grace and truth came through Jesus Christ.

Expanded Paraphrase

God truly gave the Law through Moses; and now God’s covenant grace and faithfulness have reached their climactic manifestation through Jesus Christ.

Why “But” Matters

The word “but” can quietly teach a theology.

If heard too strongly, it may imply:

  • Moses = law = bad
  • Jesus = grace = good
  • Old Testament = legalism
  • New Testament = grace
  • Torah = obstacle
  • Jesus = replacement in the sense of contradiction

That is too flat for John’s Gospel.

John’s point is not:

Moses brought the bad thing, but Jesus brought the good thing.

John’s point is closer to:

Moses truly mediated God’s gift; Jesus brings that gift to its fullness in embodied grace and truth.

Literary Context in John 1:1-18

John 1:14

“And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us… full of grace and truth.”

John has already introduced grace and truth before v. 17. The phrase describes the incarnate Word’s glory. John 1:17 then explains how this grace and truth came in history: through Jesus Christ.

John 1:16

“For from his fullness we have all received, grace upon grace.”

This verse immediately precedes John 1:17. The movement is abundance, fullness, and overflow. That makes a harsh law-versus-grace reading less likely. John is speaking of divine fullness, not divine contradiction.

John 1:17

The Law was given through Moses; grace and truth came through Jesus Christ.

John 1:18

“No one has ever seen God; the only Son / the only God… he has made him known.”

John 1:18 completes the thought: Jesus is the full revelation of God. The “Son/God” wording reflects the major textual variation in John 1:18; either way, John’s point is not that Moses was false, but that Jesus uniquely exegetes the Father.

Exodus 34 Background

Key Old Testament Background

Exodus 34:6 is central:

“The LORD, the LORD, a God merciful and gracious, slow to anger, and abounding in steadfast love and faithfulness…”

The Hebrew phrase often rendered “steadfast love and faithfulness” is:

  • חֶסֶד (hesed) – covenant love, loyal mercy, steadfast love
  • אֱמֶת (emet) – truth, faithfulness, reliability

John’s “grace and truth” likely echoes this covenant formula.

This matters because Exodus 34 is not anti-law. It is the moment where God reveals his covenant character to Moses after Israel’s golden-calf rebellion. The God who gives Torah is also the God abounding in covenant love and faithfulness.

So when John says Jesus is full of grace and truth (John 1:14) and that grace and truth came through Jesus Christ (John 1:17), he is not saying Jesus has a different character from the God of Moses. He is saying the covenant character revealed to Moses has now become flesh in Jesus.

Preaching Line

Jesus is not God changing personalities between testaments. Jesus is the covenant heart of God walking around in skin.

Moses in John’s Gospel

John does not treat Moses as the enemy of Jesus. Moses is honored, but he is not final.

Reference What John Shows
John 1:17 The Law was given through Moses; grace and truth came through Jesus Christ.
John 1:45 Philip says Jesus is the one “of whom Moses in the Law and also the prophets wrote.”
John 3:14-15 Jesus compares his lifting up to Moses lifting the serpent in the wilderness.
John 5:39-40 The Scriptures bear witness about Jesus, yet some refuse to come to him.
John 5:45-47 Moses wrote of Jesus; failure to believe Moses leads to failure to believe Jesus.
John 6:32-35 Moses did not give the final bread from heaven; the Father gives the true bread in Jesus.
John 7:19-24 Jesus critiques those who claim Moses but mishandle the Law’s intent.
John 9:28-29 The opponents claim Moses against Jesus, but John exposes their blindness.

Summary

John’s problem is not Moses. John’s problem is misreading Moses without seeing Jesus.

Theological Issue: Contrast or Fulfillment?

Bad Contrast

Avoid this:

Moses gave law, which was harsh and graceless; Jesus brought grace, which finally replaced the bad old system.

That does not do justice to:

  • Exodus 34:6 – God revealed himself to Moses as gracious and abounding in steadfast love and faithfulness.
  • Psalm 19:7-11 – the Law of the LORD is perfect, sure, right, pure, clean, true, desirable, and sweet.
  • Psalm 119:29, 64, 142, 151 – Torah is connected to grace, truth, faithfulness, and delight.
  • Romans 7:12 – Paul says the Law is holy, righteous, and good.
  • John 5:46 – Jesus says Moses wrote of him.

Better Fulfillment

Use this:

The Law through Moses was God’s gracious covenant gift; Jesus Christ is the full, embodied, climactic revelation of the grace and truth toward which Moses pointed.

Continuity and Escalation

John 1:17 is not simple opposition. It is continuity with escalation.

Through Moses Through Jesus Christ
Torah given Grace and truth came
Real revelation Full revelation
Covenant gift Covenant fullness
Witness Fulfillment
Mediated instruction Incarnate presence

Greek Summary

1. No explicit “but”

Greek has no ἀλλά, δέ, or other adversative conjunction between the clauses.

2. Asyndeton

The clauses are joined without a connector. This gives the reader context-driven parallelism rather than a forced logical contrast.

3. Different verbs

Greek Translation Significance
ἐδόθη (edothe) “was given” Divine passive; the Law is God’s gift through Moses.
ἐγένετο (egeneto) “came / came to be / happened / was realized” Grace and truth entered history climactically through Jesus Christ.

4. Same preposition

Both clauses use διά (dia, “through”):

  • The Law was given through Moses.
  • Grace and truth came through Jesus Christ.

This parallel construction strengthens the sense of comparison and keeps the wording from becoming crude opposition. The fulfillment reading comes from the wider Prologue context, not from the preposition by itself.

5. “Grace and truth” as a pair

The phrase ἡ χάρις καὶ ἡ ἀλήθεια (he charis kai he aletheia) likely functions as a covenantal pair echoing hesed ve’emet from Exodus 34:6.

Movement Structure for Teaching

1. The Law Was a Gift, Not a Mistake

Text: John 1:17a; Exodus 20:1-17; Psalm 19:7-11; Romans 7:12

The Law came through Moses as God’s gracious covenant instruction. It revealed God’s holiness, formed Israel’s identity, exposed sin, and guarded covenant life.

Teaching punch: Do not preach Moses like God made a mistake before Jesus came.

2. Grace and Truth Are God’s Covenant Character in Flesh

Text: John 1:14, 17; Exodus 34:6; Psalm 85:10; Psalm 89:14

Grace and truth are not abstractions. They are God’s covenant heart made visible in Jesus. Jesus does not merely talk about grace and truth; he embodies them.

Teaching punch: In Jesus, God’s covenant love and faithfulness have a face.

3. Jesus Is the Fulfillment, Not the Enemy, of Moses

Text: John 1:45; John 3:14-15; John 5:39-47; John 6:32-35

John’s Gospel repeatedly shows that Moses points beyond himself to Jesus. The failure is not honoring Moses; the failure is using Moses to reject the one Moses anticipated.

Teaching punch: You do not honor Moses by stopping at Moses. You honor Moses by following his witness to Christ.

Preaching Punch

  • There is no “but” in the Greek text of John 1:17.
  • That matters because “but” can make Moses and Jesus sound like rivals.
  • John does not present Torah as graceless.
  • Torah was God’s gift through Moses.
  • Jesus is the fullness of God’s covenant grace and truth.
  • The problem is not Moses. The problem is reading Moses without seeing Jesus.
  • Jesus does not reveal a different God from the God of Sinai. Jesus reveals the God of Sinai fully.

Diagnostic Questions

  1. Have I been reading John 1:17 as if Moses and Jesus are enemies?
  2. Do I treat the Old Testament as mostly law and the New Testament as mostly grace?
  3. Do I see Torah as God’s gracious gift, even if it is not God’s final revelation?
  4. Am I letting John define “grace and truth” from Exodus 34 and the whole Prologue?
  5. Where have I created a law-vs-grace contrast that Scripture itself does not require?
  6. Do I honor Moses by seeing how Moses points to Jesus?

Gospel Pivot

The gospel is not that God used to be law and now God is grace.

The gospel is that the God who gave Torah through Moses has now come near in the Word made flesh. Jesus embodies the covenant mercy and faithfulness of God. He does what Torah could witness to but could not complete: he reveals the Father fully, gives life from his fullness, and brings grace upon grace.

Conclusion Imperatives

  • Do not insert a theological war where John gives a theological climax.
  • Do not flatten Moses into legalism.
  • Do not reduce Jesus to “not Moses.”
  • Read John 1:17 with John 1:14, John 1:16, John 1:18, and Exodus 34:6.
  • Preach Jesus as the fullness of God’s covenant grace and truth.
