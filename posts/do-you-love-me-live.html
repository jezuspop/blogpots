Do You Love Me?

Jesus calls us friends. My reading of Peter’s story brings that friendship into focus: Jesus’ self-giving love invites us to respond to Him and care…

  • Valentine's Day story: Tom Pearce made it to shore after the Loch Ard wreck in 1878, then went back into the water to rescue Eva Carmichael. They were the only two survivors.
  • They did not marry. We don’t need to guess what either of them felt for the story to work.
  • Why do we feel disappointed? We're obsessed with romantic love
  • The Bible's central theme is love — because God is love (1 John 4:8)
  • The world's influence on our understanding of love can be subtle yet dangerous

Big Idea

Jesus calls us friends. My reading of Peter’s story brings that friendship into focus: Jesus’ self-giving love invites us to respond to Him and care for others.

1

Four Greek Words for Love

"A new command I give you: Love one another. As I have loved you, so you must love one another."

John 13:34 (NIV)
  • These are teaching categories, not rigid definitions for every Greek use. Agapē is a noun and phileō a verb; John 21 compares the verbs agapaō and phileō.
  • Agape: Unconditional, sacrificial love; keeps loving even when unresponsive; God is love
  • Storge: Natural affection/obligation (parental love)
  • Eros: Romance, sexual attraction; basic idea can become focused on self-satisfaction; higher when it's "our happiness" vs "my happiness"
  • Phileo: Affection and fondness, including the love we recognize in friendship
  • We learn about agape in church — Jesus on the cross defines it
  • What we hear about love through entertainment and advertising often puts sex or romance first.
  • Application: As disciples, we must be careful not to follow the world's patterns (Romans 12:2)
2

Jesus' Expectation

"When they had finished eating, Jesus said to Simon Peter, 'Simon son of John, do you love me more than these?' 'Yes, Lord,' he said, 'you know that I love you.' Jesus said, 'Feed my lambs.'"

John 21:15 (NIV)

Two Charcoal Fires

  • Peter warmed himself at a charcoal fire on the night he denied Jesus. At another charcoal fire, the risen Jesus has fish and bread waiting for the disciples. After breakfast come three questions and a charge to care for his sheep. Reading the scenes side by side brings out the restoration. John 18:15–27 · John 21:9–17
  • Jesus asks Peter twice: "Do you love (agape) me?"
  • Then the third time: "Do you love (phileo) me?"
  • In Greek, Jesus uses agapaō in his first two questions and phileō in the third. Peter uses phileō each time. John uses both verbs for the Father’s love for the Son (John 3:35; 5:20), so the change may be stylistic.
  • My reading: I hear an invitation to friendship here. Jesus’ repeated call to care for His sheep is the clearer emphasis of the passage.
  • Why?
    • Jesus takes the initiative in love and invites us to respond. Giving and receiving both belong in this friendship.
    • Friendship need not revolve around exclusivity; it can make room for others.
    • Phileo is the love everyone is called by Jesus to share — not just those dating or married
3

Called Friends, Not Servants

"My command is this: Love each other as I have loved you. Greater love has no one than this: to lay down one's life for one's friends. You are my friends if you do what I command. I no longer call you servants, because a servant does not know his master's business. Instead, I have called you friends..."

John 15:12–15 (NIV, excerpt)
  • Greater love has no one than this: to lay down one's life for one's friends
  • You are Jesus' friends if you do what He commands
  • He no longer calls us servants but friends — everything He learned from the Father, He made known to us
  • Application: This is the love Jesus challenges Peter to have after the crucifixion

Conclusion

  • Summary: What is your understanding of love? Are you caught up with eros like the world?
  • Call to Action: Take Jesus’ invitation to friendship seriously. How can you return His love and care for the people He entrusts to you?
  • Closing: Jesus wants you as His friend. Not just a servant. Not just a recipient. A friend.
