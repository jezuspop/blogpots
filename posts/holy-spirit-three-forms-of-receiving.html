Holy Spirit: Three Forms of Receiving

The Bible reveals three distinct forms of receiving the Holy Spirit; understanding the differences prevents false teaching and clarifies our salvation.

  • Study of the Holy Spirit has many parts: Who/what is the Spirit? What does the Spirit do? How do you get the Spirit?
  • This study covers the last question — directly addressing misconceptions from charismatic groups
  • Prerequisite: Solid understanding of biblical baptism
  • Goal: Show each form is distinct in scripture, pulling from prophecy and examples

Big Idea

The Bible reveals three distinct forms of receiving the Holy Spirit — understanding the differences prevents false teaching and clarifies our salvation.

1

Outpouring ("God's Spirit Poured Out")

"When the day of Pentecost came, they were all together in one place. Suddenly a sound like the blowing of a violent wind came from heaven and filled the whole house where they were sitting. They saw what seemed to be tongues of fire that separated and came to rest on each of them. All of them were filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak in other tongues as the Spirit enabled them."

Acts 2:1-4 (NIV)
  • What is it? God's Spirit pouring onto people (prophesied in Isaiah 44:3, Zechariah 12:10, Joel 2:28-29)
  • Purpose: God's way of highlighting significant events — happens only twice in Acts:
    • The beginning of the church (Acts 2:1-4)
    • The acceptance of the Gentiles (Acts 10:44-45)
  • How received: Pouring over/immersing groups of people
  • Charismatic groups call this "Baptism of the Holy Spirit"
2

Indwelling ("God's Spirit Dwelling in Us")

"Peter replied, 'Repent and be baptized, every one of you, in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins. And you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. The promise is for you and your children and for all who are far off—for all whom the Lord our God will call.'"

Acts 2:38-39 (NIV)
  • What is it? God's Spirit dwelling in us (prophesied in Ezekiel 36:24-27)
  • Confirmed in 1 Corinthians 3:16, 6:19; Romans 8:9; Ephesians 2:22; Galatians 4:6
  • Purpose: Salvation, forgiveness of sins, indwelling of Holy Spirit
  • How received: Through water baptism (Acts 2:38, 8:36-38, 10:47)
  • This is the standard for all Christians — the only explicit command about receiving the Holy Spirit
3

Transferring ("The Laying of Hands")

"When the apostles in Jerusalem heard that Samaria had accepted the word of God, they sent Peter and John to Samaria... Then Peter and John placed their hands on them, and they received the Holy Spirit."

Acts 8:14-17 (NIV)
  • What is it? Holy Spirit being passed through laying on of hands, leading to miraculous gifts
  • Purpose: The signs in Acts confirm the message (Acts 14:3; Hebrews 2:3–4). Mark 16:15–18 belongs to the longer ending, absent from some early manuscripts. I read the signs as descriptions, not instructions for believers to attempt.
  • How received: Laying of hands by apostles (Acts 6:3-6, 8:14-19, 19:5-6, Romans 1:11-12, 2 Timothy 1:6)
  • Acts 8 and 19 describe these events after baptism.
  • Not everyone received this — apostles weren't present at every baptism (Acts 8:36-39 — the Ethiopian Eunuch)
4

The Indwelling Is The Standard

"You, however, are not in the realm of the flesh but are in the realm of the Spirit, if indeed the Spirit of God lives in you. And if anyone does not have the Spirit of Christ, they do not belong to Christ."

Romans 8:9 (NIV)
  • The only explicit command about receiving the Holy Spirit is Acts 2:38
  • There can only be one baptism (Ephesians 4:4-6)
  • The outpouring shows up too few times to be the normal way
  • The miraculous gifts would come to an end (1 Corinthians 13:8-10)
  • Church fathers (Origen, Augustine, Chrysostom) confirmed miracles diminished after apostolic age
  • Application: The Holy Spirit still works through us today — appearing through the prayers of faithful Christians (James 5:13-18)

Conclusion

  • Summary: Scripture points to receiving the indwelling as the norm for salvation
  • Call to Action: Understand the biblical teaching on the Holy Spirit — don't be swayed by confusing or false teachings
  • Closing: The miraculous gifts ended, but the Holy Spirit continues to work powerfully through faithful prayer.
