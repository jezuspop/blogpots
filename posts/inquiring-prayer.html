Topical Sermons November 24, 2026

Inquiring Prayer

Inquiring of the Lord was serious business in the Old Testament.

  • Theme: Prayer
  • Looking at different types of ways to pray
  • Today: Inquiring Prayer—more teaching than normal, but hopefully stirring and inspiring

Big Idea

Inquiring of the Lord was serious business in the Old Testament. Jesus and the New Testament writers call us to seek God with that same seriousness: take the initiative, trust Him, obey His Word, and check your motives.

1

What Is Inquiring Prayer? (Exodus 33:7–11)

  • Inquiring of the Lord means seeking His guidance. Exodus 33:7–11 gives us a picture: Moses pitched the tent of meeting outside the camp, and anyone seeking the Lord could go there. The pillar of cloud came down at the entrance when Moses entered.
  • Other accounts involve priests, prophets, or urgent decisions. In 1 Samuel 23:9–12, David seeks guidance while Saul is pursuing him.
2

The Sacred Items (Exodus 28:6–30)

The Ephod

  • The ephod was a priestly garment. The breastpiece fastened to it held twelve stones representing the tribes (Exodus 28:6–30).
  • Think of the Infinity Gauntlet for a visual, not a theology lesson: unlike a magic object, the ephod didn’t control God.
  • David said "Bring the ephod" when he learned Saul was plotting against him (1 Samuel 23:9)
  • Gideon made an ephod that became a snare when Israel went after it (Judges 8:27). That is a caution about treating a sacred object as an end in itself.

David’s Questions at Keilah

  • David asked Abiathar the priest to bring the ephod, then asked two concrete questions: Would Saul come, and would the town hand David and his men over? Once he had an answer, he left. The story shows the questions he asked without describing the ritual in detail. 1 Samuel 23:6–14

The Urim and Thummim

  • Exodus 28:30 places them in Aaron’s breastpiece of judgment. Numbers 27:21 associates the Urim with seeking the Lord’s decision through the priest.
  • Scripture does not explain the mechanics. Acts 1:26 describes casting lots to choose Judas’s replacement without identifying those lots as the Urim and Thummim.

How Do We Use It Today?

  • The NT gives guidelines pointing us in the right direction
  • Jesus’ language of asking and seeking gives us a way to apply the same posture. Bring God ordinary decisions, not just life-and-death ones.
3

Take Initiative (Matthew 7:7–11)

"Ask and it will be given to you; seek and you will find; knock and the door will be opened to you. For everyone who asks receives; the one who seeks finds; and to the one who knocks, the door will be opened."

Matthew 7:7-8 (NIV)
  • This is how I’m applying the old picture of inquiring: Jesus tells us to ask, seek, and knock.
  • Jesus repeats it for a reason. Seek God and keep asking!
4

Approach with Faith/Belief (James 1:5–8)

"If any of you lacks wisdom, you should ask God, who gives generously to all without finding fault, and it will be given to you. But when you ask, you must believe and not doubt, because the one who doubts is like a wave of the sea, blown and tossed by the wind."

James 1:5-6 (NIV)
  • God gives generously to all without finding fault
  • But when we ask, we must BELIEVE!
  • James is talking about asking for wisdom. Do I trust that God gives generously, or am I torn about whether I will listen to Him?
  • There are many times I'll throw out a hail-mary prayer and not really believe it—that's how I look before God!
5

Hold to God's Word (1 John 3:21–24)

"Dear friends, if our hearts do not condemn us, we have confidence before God and receive from him anything we ask, because we keep his commands and do what pleases him."

1 John 3:21-22 (NIV)
  • Following and obeying God's word affects your prayers being answered
  • John connects confidence with keeping His commands and doing what pleases Him. This isn’t a formula for making God grant our wishes; see 1 John 5:14.
  • "Doing what pleases God" is highly convicting
  • Matthew 7:21-23—people confident in their own righteousness but sadly mistaken on Judgment Day
  • We must "find out what pleases the Lord" (Ephesians 5:10)
6

Don't Be A Hypocrite (James 4:1–10)

"When you ask, you do not receive, because you ask with wrong motives, that you may spend what you get on your pleasures... Submit yourselves, then, to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you. Come near to God and he will come near to you."

James 4:3, 7-8 (NIV)
  • The way we live and what we want matter when we come to God.
  • Saul did inquire and received no answer before turning to the medium at Endor (1 Samuel 28:5–7). The Chronicler sums up his unfaithfulness as failing to keep the Lord’s word and failing to inquire of Him (1 Chronicles 10:13–14).
  • James implores us to get our hearts right:
    • Humble ourselves (get open)
    • Submit to God (via His word)
    • Resist Satan (repentance)
    • Wash our hands and purify our hearts
    • Grieve, mourn, and wail (get serious)
  • The promise: He will lift us up!

Conclusion

  • Summary: Understand the history of Inquiring God and apply it today
  • Call to Action: Take Initiative (Seek/Ask), Approach with Faith, Hold to God's Word, Don't Be a Hypocrite
  • Closing: Stay repentant and check your motives—this will transform your prayer life

Brief Study Notes

  • Exodus 28:6–30; 33:7–11; 1 Samuel 28:5–7; 1 Chronicles 10:13–14: The passages distinguish the clothing from the breastpiece and the fact that Saul asked from the Chronicler’s judgment of his unfaithfulness.
