Getting Back to Struggling

Struggling is truly fighting with all you have and is crucial for spiritual survival.

  • I keep coming back to what it means to fight for faith.
  • My question: How is the fight going?
  • We live in a world where people don't want struggle — it can be embarrassing
  • Picture a butterfly emerging from its chrysalis or an animal finding its way in the wild. The picture is about effort, not a rule that helping always does harm; this sermon calls us to help each other struggle.

Big Idea

Struggling is truly fighting with all you have and is crucial for spiritual survival. We need to get back to struggling with God, struggling together, and struggling with sin.

1

Struggling with God (Genesis 32:22–32)

"So Jacob was left alone, and a man wrestled with him till daybreak. When the man saw that he could not overpower him, he touched the socket of Jacob's hip so that his hip was wrenched as he wrestled... Then the man said, 'Your name will no longer be Jacob, but Israel, because you have struggled with God and with humans and have overcome.'"

Genesis 32:24-28 (NIV)

The Night Before Esau

  • Jacob hears that Esau is coming with 400 men. Afraid, he prays, sends gifts ahead, and moves his family across the Jabbok. The wrestling happens in the middle of that crisis. At dawn Jacob has a blessing and a limp. Genesis 32
  • "Struggle" here means to contend or contest with
  • Determined to fight (v. 23-24): Jacob sent his family and possessions across the stream and was left alone. Think of setting distractions aside to give God your full attention.
  • Giving your best (v. 25): We often give in and call it struggling. Jacob kept wrestling; the man touched his hip and wrenched it.
    • Hebrews 12:2-4 — In our struggle against sin, we need to give everything to overcoming
  • Not stopping until you see change (v. 26-27): Jacob wouldn’t let go without a blessing (v. 26); the blessing comes in v. 29.
  • Given a new name (v. 28): The story links Israel with struggling with God and people and prevailing.
    • Do you think that's an insult? Did he walk around ashamed? No — it was a badge of victory, courage, honor!
  • Question: Do you take your life situations to God this way? Ready to struggle with all your effort and might?
2

Struggling Together (Romans 15:30; Hebrews 3:12–14)

"I urge you, brothers and sisters, by our Lord Jesus Christ and by the love of the Spirit, to join me in my struggle by praying to God for me."

Romans 15:30 (NIV)

What Paul Asked Them to Pray For

  • Paul was headed to Jerusalem with an offering from believers in Macedonia and Achaia. He asked the Christians in Rome to pray for his safety in Judea and for the gift to be welcomed. They could take part in work happening far from home by praying with him. Romans 15:25–33
  • Paul calls the church to strive alongside him. In this case, prayer is how they join the struggle.
  • Paul appeals by the Lord Jesus Christ and the love of the Spirit.
  • Some of my closest friendships grew out of showing up for one another through early-morning prayer, fasting, and difficult seasons. Those relationships fuel my devotion to Jesus.
  • Hebrews 3:12-14 — Encourage one another daily so that none of you may be hardened by sin's deceitfulness
  • Question: Do you join the struggle with others? Are you helping those who are hurting?
3

Struggling with Sin (Ephesians 6:10–18)

"For our struggle is not against flesh and blood, but against the rulers, against the authorities, against the powers of this dark world and against the spiritual forces of evil in the heavenly realms."

Ephesians 6:12 (NIV)
  • Paul uses the picture of wrestling. He’s clear that people aren’t the enemy.
  • I can use "struggling" loosely or deceptively
  • Personal: I used to say I was “struggling with anger” even when I was just giving in to it. Calling it a struggle didn’t make it one. I wasn’t fighting my sin like an opponent in a UFC octagon.
  • Question: Are you truly struggling with...
    • Pressures at school to follow the crowd?
    • Pride at home to rebel against parents?
    • Anger that separates your marriage?
    • Bad behavior in the workplace where convictions are tested?
    • Fear to share your faith?
    • Fear to study the Bible and give up your life to God?

"During the days of Jesus' life on earth, he offered up prayers and petitions with loud cries and tears to the one who could save him from death... Although he was a son, he learned obedience from what he suffered."

Hebrews 5:7-8 (NIV)
  • Hebrews 5:9 calls Jesus the source of eternal salvation for those who obey Him. What will your struggle lead to?

Conclusion

  • Summary: Get back to struggling — with God like Jacob, together like Paul's community, and with sin like a warrior in battle
  • Call to Action: Identify where you've stopped truly struggling and start fighting again with everything you have.
  • Closing: Struggling is not something to be ashamed of — it's the badge of those who overcome.

Brief Study Notes

  • Genesis 32:22–32; Romans 15:30; Ephesians 6:10–18; Hebrews 5:7–10: Follow the passages from Jacob’s persistence to prayer with others, resistance to sin, and Jesus’ obedience.
