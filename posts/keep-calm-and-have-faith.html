Topical Sermons November 24, 2026

Keep Calm and Have Faith

Faith is confidence and conviction about the unseen; we get it from God's Word and we use it through action. Little or no action means little to no faith.

  • Faith is one of the most misunderstood concepts of Christianity
  • Sometimes we hear faith described as believing whatever you want, even if you can’t prove it.
  • But the Bible's definition is much more than that!
  • Imagine key Bible events without faith: Would Noah have built the ark? Would Abraham have offered Isaac? Would Moses have led the people through the sea? Hebrews 11 shows what trust led people to do, and Hebrews 12:2 points us to Jesus enduring the cross.

Big Idea

Faith is confidence and conviction about the unseen — we get it from God's Word and we use it through action. Little or no action means little to no faith.

1

What Is Faith? (Hebrews 11:1–3)

"Now faith is confidence in what we hope for and assurance about what we do not see."

Hebrews 11:1 (NIV)
  • Different translations reveal deeper meaning:
  • NASB: "Faith is assurance of things hoped for, the conviction of things not seen"
    • Having conviction about the unseen
    • Example: My conviction in purity has kept me faithful even in weakest moments
  • Amplified: "Faith perceiving as real fact what is not revealed to the senses"
    • I picture it like Spidey sense: faith changes what you notice and how you respond. That’s my illustration, not a biblical definition.
  • The Message: "This trust in God is the firm foundation under everything that makes life worth living. It's our handle on what we can't see"
    • For me, trust in God gives life purpose and direction. What would change if I stopped trusting Him?
2

Where Do I Get It? (Romans 10:14–17)

"Consequently, faith comes from hearing the message, and the message is heard through the word about Christ."

Romans 10:17 (NIV)
  • In Romans 10:14–17, Paul is talking about hearing the message about Christ. Reading your Bible is one way to keep listening to that message.
  • 2 Peter 1:5-9: Grow it by addition:
    • Faith → Goodness → Knowledge → Self-control → Perseverance → Godliness → Mutual Affection → Love
  • These qualities keep you from being ineffective and unproductive
  • Without them, you're nearsighted and blind
3

How Do I Use It? (James 2:14–17)

"What good is it, dear brothers and sisters, if you say you have faith but don't show it by your actions? Can that kind of faith save anyone?... So you see, faith by itself isn't enough. Unless it produces good deeds, it is dead and useless."

James 2:14, 17 (NLT)
  • Put it into practice — if you have faith, show action
  • Example: James pictures seeing a brother or sister without clothes or food and offering good wishes without help (James 2:15–16). What good is that?
  • Self-check:
    • Loving God — Are you having consistent times reading and praying?
    • Raising children to follow Jesus — What are you doing to teach them at home?
    • Husbands leading spiritually — Do you leave it to your wife?
    • Helping one another stay faithful — Getting with people consistently?
    • Investing in youth ministry — Are you actively involved?
    • Giving to God — Do you give faithfully?
    • Growing the church — Are you active in sharing?
  • Truth: "Little or no action means little to no faith"
  • Where would the words “you of little faith” land in my life? (Compare Matthew 14:31.)

Conclusion

  • Summary: Be confident in what faith is. Get it from the Source (God's Word). Use it for God (action).
  • Call to Action: Examine your faith this week. Make changes that will build it.
  • Closing: Keep Calm and Have Faith.

Brief Study Notes

  • Hebrews 11:7, 17–19, 29; 12:2; Romans 10:14–17; James 2:14–17: Follow the difference between describing faith and showing what trust does.
