Topical Sermons November 24, 2026

Christian Identity

Our true identity is found not by looking outward to community or inward to feelings, but upward to God; hidden in Christ.

  • Think about the things we buy and what they promise to say about us. Holiday shopping is one place that shows up. Do we want the gift itself, or the feeling that getting or giving it proves something about us?
  • What does this say about us? Why do we spend so much?
  • Timothy Keller's book "Making Sense of God" offers insights on identity formation
  • Our spending may be tied to how we view ourselves

Big Idea

Our true identity is found not by looking outward to community or inward to feelings, but upward to God — hidden in Christ.

1

Understanding Identity

"For you died, and your life is now hidden with Christ in God."

Colossians 3:3 (NIV)
  • Working definition, drawing from Keller: Identity is a sense of self that holds across different settings.
  • We live in many spheres: Christian, husband, father, son, evangelist, artist, fan
  • The durable self is what's true of you in every setting — the "you" that sticks around
  • Without it, you'd be various masks for different occasions with no actual face behind them
  • Question: What about you does not change from place to place?
2

Two Trains of Thought on Identity

"Do not conform to the pattern of this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind."

Romans 12:2 (NIV, excerpt)
  • Looking outward: Some people define themselves mainly through community, family, or religion.
    • Pro: Great encouragement and feedback
    • Con: Imposing and crushing; no voice ("My only ambition is to please my parents")
  • Looking inward: Others define themselves mainly through personal feelings or convictions.
    • Pro: Discover your own values; can change your view
    • Con: Relying on feelings; identity subject to change; no objective view ("I don't care what everyone thinks — I love myself")
  • Christians are subject to the same patterns — but God's way is more fulfilling
3

Paul's Answer — Look Upward

"So look at Apollos and me as mere servants of Christ who have been put in charge of explaining God's mysteries... As for me, it matters very little how I might be evaluated by you or by any human authority. I don't even trust my own judgment on this point. It is the Lord himself who will examine me and decide."

1 Corinthians 4:1–4 (NLT, selected excerpts)

Cultural Background: A Steward

  • In Jesus’ illustration, a steward manages another person’s household and gives the servants their food allowance. Paul uses the same word for the role he and Apollos have been given. The message belongs to God, and the steward is judged by faithfulness, not popularity. Luke 12:42–44 · 1 Corinthians 4:1–6
  • The Corinthian church had an identity crisis — divisions over which leader they liked best
  • In chapter 3, Paul and Apollos are servants with different roles; God gives the growth.
  • Paul's source of identity: Not from the group, not from his own thoughts — but from God
  • He didn't look outward nor inward but UPWARD
  • That's what everyone is called to when it comes to the cross
4

The Cross Calls Us Upward

"For this is how God loved the world: He gave his one and only Son, so that everyone who believes in him will not perish but have eternal life. God sent his Son into the world not to judge the world, but to save the world through him."

John 3:16-17 (NLT)
  • In John 3:13, Jesus speaks as the Son of Man who came down from heaven. He is the one who can speak with authority about heavenly things.
  • Just as Moses lifted the bronze snake in the wilderness, so the Son of Man must be lifted up
  • Our true identity is hidden in Christ (Colossians 3:3)
  • Let Jesus, the author and perfecter of our faith, define our identity (Hebrews 12:2)

Conclusion

  • Summary: As we take communion, remember where our true identity is — hidden in Christ
  • Call to Action: Fight to look upward to Him for validation, encouragement, and hope
  • Closing: Let Jesus define who you are. Not the world. Not even yourself. Jesus.
