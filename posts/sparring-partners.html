Topical Sermons November 24, 2026

Sparring Partners

Great sparring times produce the world's best fighters; when we engage in healthy discipling times with each other, we'll fight the true battle…

  • Theme for the year: "Fight"
  • Theme for this month: Iron Sharpens Iron
  • Life is hard and we need to "FIGHT"—but the fight is on a spiritual level, not physical
  • This lesson provides practical tools for healthy discipling relationships

Big Idea

Great sparring times produce the world's best fighters—when we engage in healthy discipling times with each other, we'll fight the true battle against Satan rather than fighting one another.

Why We Need Sparring Partners

Paul identifies the real struggle

For our struggle is not against flesh and blood, but against the rulers, against the authorities, against the powers of this dark world and against the spiritual forces of evil in the heavenly realms.

Ephesians 6:12 (NIV)
Ephesians 6:12

Old Testament Background: Whose Armor?

  • In Isaiah, God puts on righteousness as a breastplate and salvation as a helmet when no one else intervenes. Ephesians echoes that picture and calls believers to stand. The people we’re helping stand alongside us; they aren’t the enemy. Isaiah 59:15–17 · Ephesians 6:10–18
  • We face battles we cannot physically solve: hurt relationships, failed marriages, vicious thinking, addiction, hopelessness, fear, pride
  • We need Iron Sharpening relationships to win these spiritual battles
1

Sparring Is Not Fighting

The goal of Paul’s charge to Timothy

The goal of this command is love, which comes from a pure heart and a good conscience and a sincere faith.

1 Timothy 1:5 (NIV)
  • You’re here to sharpen each other, not to win. The person across from you is your partner, not your opponent.
  • When someone with superior skills knocks out their opponent in sparring, nobody wins
  • Our times shouldn't be about arguing or bullying one another
  • The goal of training/sparring is LOVE
  • When we’re emotionally wound up, it’s easy to go in ready to argue instead of ready to listen.

Practical

  • Remind yourself that the goal is love, no matter what comes your way
2

Come Prepared

Paul’s charge to preach with patience

Preach the word; be prepared in season and out of season; correct, rebuke and encourage—with great patience and careful instruction.

2 Timothy 4:2 (NIV)
  • Think of the ring instruction, “Protect yourself at all times.” In a discipling conversation, preparation starts with prayer.
  • It's emotionally, mentally, and spiritually risky to disciple/spar without preparation
  • Paul charged Timothy to be ready to preach, correct, rebuke, and encourage, with patience and careful instruction. For our conversations, that means preparing how we’ll listen as well as what we’ll say.
  • If we cannot be prepared in our sparring times with each other, how will we be prepared during battles in the real world?

Practical

  • Pray before you meet—pray to be humble to take input, pray to be loving to give input
3

Get Advice Before You Get Stuck

Jesus speaks what the Father commanded

For I did not speak on my own, but the Father who sent me commanded me to say all that I have spoken. I know that his command leads to eternal life. So whatever I say is just what the Father has told me to say.

John 12:49–50 (NIV)
  • The sparring image makes the point: someone with experience can spot what both partners are missing.
  • GET ADVICE! We need input on situations to make our times productive
  • Jesus says he spoke what the Father commanded, not on his own authority. That is more than taking advice. The application for us: don’t assume we always know what needs to be said.
  • We should be getting input all the time

Practical

  • Get input on your discipling times; sit in and watch others—learn from their techniques
4

Put On Faith, Love, and Hope

Faith, hope, and love that endure

Three things will last forever—faith, hope, and love—and the greatest of these is love.

1 Corinthians 13:13 (NLT)
A picture of faith, love, and hope in practice

We remember before our God and Father your work produced by faith, your labor prompted by love, and your endurance inspired by hope in our Lord Jesus Christ.

1 Thessalonians 1:3 (NIV)
  • We are clothed with Christ (Galatians 3:27)—His attitude, teaching, and principles
  • Work Produced by Faith: Embrace the work ahead led by faith in God's power to change
  • Labor Prompted by Love: Be willing to offer and receive difficult input because you love the person, never to wound them.
  • Endurance Inspired by Hope: Endure training because of hope in what God can do

Practical Questions

  • Faith: Was I faithful while helping this person? Am I faithful about the advice given?
  • Love: Was I loving? Did I show appreciation for them trying?
  • Hope: Did I provide the hope God has for this person?

Conclusion

  • Summary: Apply these four tips—Sparring is Not Fighting, Come Prepared, Get Advice, Put On Faith, Love, and Hope
  • Call to Action: Implement healthy sparring/discipling times in your relationships
  • Closing: Great sparring produces great fighters—when we sharpen one another, we'll fight the true battle against Satan and his demons
