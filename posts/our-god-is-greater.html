Topical Sermons November 24, 2026

Our God Is Greater

God is greater than what’s in front of us. In Acts, neither inexperience, tragedy, sin, nor prejudice stopped him from working.

  • Theme: By Faith—Faith in Action
  • In prepping, I've been reading Acts—it's been good to see how amazing God really is
  • I’ve had seasons that were some of the best and the hardest at the same time.
  • The good should tell me that God is Greater than my problems, discouragements, or setbacks

Big Idea

God is greater than what’s in front of us. In Acts, neither inexperience, tragedy, sin, nor prejudice stopped him from working.

Foundation: The Spirit Empowers the Witnesses

Jesus’s commission before the ascension

But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit comes on you; and you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth.

Acts 1:8 (NIV)
  • In Acts, God would repeatedly overcome major problems because He is God!
  • Take a moment and write down the most troubling thing you're facing right now
  • My hope is that by the end, you'll be inspired about how Great God is!
1

God Is Greater Than Our Inexperience

The response to Peter at Pentecost

Those who accepted his message were baptized, and about three thousand were added to their number that day.

Acts 2:41 (NIV)
  • After Jesus ascended, the disciples could no longer turn to him as they had before. He had promised them the Holy Spirit (Acts 1:8–11).
  • These were still the disciples who had stumbled. Now they were being asked to carry the message forward.
  • Jesus was sending them as witnesses to the ends of the earth. What would be going through your mind?
  • Did their inexperience derail God’s plan? No! About three thousand received Peter’s message and were baptized.

Personal Story

  • Losing a close friend and mentor made it hard to imagine ministry without him. But who did my mentor turn to? God! The same God can work through me without the person I leaned on.

Challenge

  • Decide to believe God can work in your life despite lack of leadership or inexperience
2

God Is Greater Than Personal Tragedy

The gospel spreads after Stephen’s death

Those who had been scattered preached the word wherever they went... So there was great joy in that city.

Acts 8:4, 8 (NIV)
  • Stephen was killed, the church mourned, and persecution scattered believers (Acts 7:54–8:4).
  • The tragedy was real. It also didn’t stop the scattered believers from preaching the word or stop joy from reaching a Samaritan city.

Personal Story

  • After several losses close to home, God’s greatness wasn’t the first thing on my mind. Grief raised hard questions. Those losses were real, and I still believe God is greater than them.

Challenge

  • Decide to believe God can work in your life despite personal tragedy
3

God Is Greater Than Our Own Sin

Jesus commissions Saul through Ananias

But the Lord said to Ananias, 'Go! This man is my chosen instrument to proclaim my name to the Gentiles and their kings and to the people of Israel.'

Acts 9:15 (NIV)
  • Saul thought he was right to oppose Jesus. He persecuted Jesus’ followers and approved of Stephen’s killing (Acts 8:1–3; 9:1–2; 26:9–11).
  • Yet God still converts this guy!
  • Sometimes we think God can't use us because of our sin or others' sin
  • If God can change the heart of a man who helped persecute the church, I think anything is possible.
  • Look at what Paul did for the early church despite his sin!

Challenge

  • Decide to believe God can work in your life despite your sin or others' sin
4

God Is Greater Than Our Prejudice

Peter speaks in Cornelius’s house

Then Peter began to speak: 'I now realize how true it is that God does not show favoritism but accepts from every nation the one who fears him and does what is right.'

Acts 10:34–35 (NIV)
  • Peter had to recognize that he must not treat Gentiles as unclean. The believers with him were astonished when the Holy Spirit was poured out on Gentiles too (Acts 10:28, 44–48).
  • Jesus had already told his disciples to make disciples of all nations (Matthew 28:19). What assumptions kept them from seeing what that meant?
  • Cornelius was a Roman centurion, and Acts introduces him as God-fearing (Acts 10:1–2).
  • God was greater than their prejudice. Peter ordered that Cornelius and the others be baptized.

Personal Story

  • I had to change my perception of moving to Fresno—not high on my dream destinations. But God is Greater! We're changing the perception in campus and singles about Fresno.

Challenge

  • Decide to believe God can work in your life despite your perceptions or prejudices

Conclusion

  • Summary: God Is Greater than our lack of leadership, personal tragedy, our sin, and our perceptions
  • Call to Action: See God Greater than the issues in your life today
  • Closing: If you're visiting or studying, make the decision to become a Christian despite your perceptions—God is Greater!
