Increase Our Faith: Struggle Increases Faith

Trials will purify and increase our faith like gold refined.

  • Theme this year: By Faith — series to help us grow in our faith
  • Last week: Knowing God's word increases our faith
  • Today: Persevering through trials — how struggling increases our faith
  • Picture a butterfly emerging from its chrysalis or an animal making its way in the wild. Effort is not always a sign something is wrong.
  • My challenge: Get better at struggling

Big Idea

Trials will purify and increase our faith like gold refined. We need to get better at struggling — struggling for our relationship with God, against the sin of this world, and together as God's people.

1

Trials Purify Faith Like Gold (1 Peter 1:3–9)

"These trials will show that your faith is genuine. It is being tested as fire tests and purifies gold—though your faith is far more precious than mere gold. So when your faith remains strong through many trials, it will bring you much praise and glory and honor on the day when Jesus Christ is revealed…"

1 Peter 1:7 (NLT, excerpt)
  • Peter paints the picture of Christianity: While on earth, we will have trials of many kinds
  • These trials show our faith is genuine — tested like gold refined
  • Peter’s picture: Fire tests and refines gold. Trials can show what our faith is made of, and our faith matters more than gold.
2

Struggling for Our Relationship with God (Genesis 32:22–32)

"This left Jacob all alone in the camp, and a man came and wrestled with him until the dawn began to break. When the man saw that he would not win the match, he touched Jacob's hip and wrenched it out of its socket. Then the man said, 'Let me go, for the dawn is breaking!' But Jacob said, 'I will not let you go unless you bless me.'"

Genesis 32:24–26 (NLT)

The Night Before Esau

  • Jacob hears that Esau is coming with 400 men. Afraid, he prays, sends gifts ahead, and moves his family across the Jabbok. The wrestling happens in the middle of that crisis. At dawn Jacob has a blessing and a limp. Genesis 32
  • Determined to fight (v. 23-24): Jacob sent his family and possessions across the stream and was left alone. Give God your undivided attention.
  • Giving your best (v. 25): The man touched Jacob’s hip and wrenched it. Jacob didn’t let go.
    • E. M. Bounds in The Weapon of Prayer, ch. 1: don’t make prayer secondary; let it engage you fully.
    • We can't have mediocre prayers and call it wrestling!
  • Not stopping until you see change (v. 26-27): Jacob refused to let go without a blessing (v. 26); he was blessed in v. 29.
  • A new name and a blessing (vv. 28–29): The story links the name Israel with Jacob struggling with God and people and prevailing. Then Jacob receives a blessing. What a badge of honor!
  • Challenge: Find your Peniel — make it your place to wrestle with God
3

Struggling Against the Sin of This World (Ephesians 6:10–18)

"For our struggle is not against flesh and blood, but against the rulers, against the authorities, against the powers of this dark world and against the spiritual forces of evil in the heavenly realms."

Ephesians 6:12 (NIV)
  • Paul pictures a wrestling match. The next distinction matters: people are not the enemy.
  • Paul brings to light the reality: It cannot be taken lightly
  • Personal: I can feel unprepared when I’m tempted or surprised by my own anger. Saying “I can’t believe they did that” makes it easy to miss the fight going on in me.
  • What should I expect? I am in a WAR! Demonic forces and dark worlds!
  • No one goes to war and thinks, "I can't believe he shot at me like that!"
  • The world is not going to warn you or apologize for attacking you
  • Challenge: Picture the spiritual battlefield when you struggle with the world
4

Struggling Together as God's People (Romans 15:30; Hebrews 3:12–14)

"I urge you, brothers and sisters, by our Lord Jesus Christ and by the love of the Spirit, to join me in my struggle by praying to God for me."

Romans 15:30 (NIV)

What Paul Asked Them to Pray For

  • Paul was headed to Jerusalem with an offering from believers in Macedonia and Achaia. He asked the Christians in Rome to pray for his safety in Judea and for the gift to be welcomed. They could take part in work happening far from home by praying with him. Romans 15:25–33
  • Paul asks for people to strive alongside him. Here, they fight at his side by praying.
  • Paul wanted the church to strive together with him — bonding them to Jesus
  • Personal: Some of my closest friendships formed while we ran, worked out, camped, or prayed together. Those are my struggle buddies, and they fuel my devotion to Jesus.
  • Hebrews 3:12-14 — Encourage one another daily so none of you may be hardened by sin's deceitfulness
  • Challenge: Identify your "Struggle Buddies" and spend consistent time with them

Conclusion

  • Summary: Get better at struggling — for your relationship with God, against the sin of this world, and together as God's people
  • Call to Action: Find your Peniel, picture the battlefield, and identify your struggle buddies.
  • Closing: In so doing, we will increase our faith like Peter, whose words still call the church to endure. Early Christian tradition remembers his faithful death (Eusebius, Church History 2.25)..

Brief Study Notes

  • Genesis 32:24–29; 1 Peter 1:7: Jacob’s callout uses the NLT opener; the gold picture stays with Peter’s metaphor.
