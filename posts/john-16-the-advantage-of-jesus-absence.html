John 16: The Advantage of Jesus’ Absence

Why does Jesus say his departure is an advantage? Follow the Spirit’s work, the disciples’ grief, and the promise of joy.

Contextual qualifications for the Ascension application

The Ascension connection is a useful canonical synthesis. In John 16, Jesus’ departure includes the approaching death, resurrection, and return to the Father; it should not be reduced to the Ascension event alone. - The promise to guide into truth is spoken first to Jesus’ disciples in the Farewell Discourse. Its ongoing relevance to the church does not grant every believer the apostles’ eyewitness role or make every private impression infallible. - The Spirit is another Advocate (14:16), distinguished from the Father and Son. Saying the Spirit mediates Christ’s presence does not mean the Spirit is simply Jesus under a different name. - Jesus remains the incarnate, risen Lord. The teaching contrast between local embodied ministry and worldwide Spirit-enabled presence is not a claim that Jesus stops having a body or that his divine power was previously confined by geography. - The imagined thoughts assigned to Mary and Jesus in the illustration below are homiletical reconstruction, not reported speech from John 20:17. The meaning of “do not hold on to me” is debated, and Luke 7:28 does not explicitly explain kingdom greatness in terms of the Ascension. - The disciples’ sorrow turning to joy does not mean every personal sorrow quickly disappears or that grieving Christians lack the Spirit.

Study questions: What does Jesus explicitly promise, to whom, and for what mission? Which further connections come from Acts, Paul, or Hebrews rather than John 16 itself?

Commentary Notes

Introduction & Context

John 16 sits near the end of the Farewell Discourse (John 13-17). Jesus has washed the disciples’ feet (John 13:1-17), announced betrayal and denial (John 13:18-38), comforted troubled hearts (John 14:1-31), called them to abide in him (John 15:1-17), and warned them about the world’s hatred (John 15:18-27). Now he speaks directly about what his departure will mean.

The emotional problem is obvious: the disciples think Jesus’ absence will make them weaker.

Jesus says the opposite:

It is to your advantage that I go away. – John 16:7

That statement is the hinge of the chapter and the bridge into RS’s Ascension lesson. The disciples imagine loss. Jesus reveals advantage. They imagine distance. Jesus reveals Spirit-mediated nearness. They imagine abandonment. Jesus reveals a deeper presence than physical proximity could ever provide.

Big Idea

Jesus’ departure is not divine absence. Through the Ascension and the sending of the Spirit, Jesus becomes more available, more internal, and more missionally present to every disciple.

Driving Question

What if Jesus going away was not the loss of access, but the beginning of greater access?

Key Themes

  1. Jesus prepares disciples for pressure – John 16:1-4
    • Jesus warns them ahead of time so suffering does not become scandal.
  2. The departure of Jesus is advantageous – John 16:7
    • The Ascension is not Jesus becoming unavailable. It is Jesus becoming available by the Spirit.
  3. The Spirit continues Jesus’ ministry – John 16:8-15
    • The Spirit exposes the world and guides the disciples.
  4. The Spirit is Christ-centered – John 16:14
    • The Spirit glorifies Jesus, not himself.
  5. Sorrow is real but temporary – John 16:20-22
    • The disciples’ grief will be transformed into joy.
  6. Prayer access is intensified after Jesus’ work – John 16:23-27
    • The disciples will ask the Father in Jesus’ name.
  7. The disciples will fail, but Jesus will not – John 16:31-32
    • They will scatter, yet the Father remains with Jesus.
  8. Peace comes from Jesus’ victory, not easy circumstances – John 16:33
    • Trouble remains, but the world has already been overcome.

Ascension Blend

RS’s Ascension Key Thought

Through the Ascension, Jesus offers something greater than physical presence – He becomes infinitely available to everyone through the Holy Spirit.

John 16 supplies the theological engine for that claim:

“It is to your advantage that I go away…” – John 16:7

The Ascension Lesson in John 16 Language

Best Teaching Line

The disciples thought the Ascension meant losing Jesus. Jesus says the Ascension is how he becomes impossible to lose.

RS’s Notes: John 16 and the Ascension

One-Sentence Sermon Summary

Jesus tells sorrowful disciples that his departure is not the end of nearness but the beginning of a greater nearness through the Spirit.

Sermon Title Options

  • The Advantage of Jesus’ Absence
  • Better Than Holding On
  • When Jesus Goes Away
  • The Ascension Advantage
  • Impossible to Lose

Best Title

The Advantage of Jesus’ Absence

1. The Advantage of His Departure (John 16:5-7)

> “But because I have said these things to you, sorrow has filled your heart. Nevertheless, I tell you the truth: it is to your advantage that I go away…” – John 16:6-7****

Main Point: Jesus’ departure feels like loss, but he insists it is advantage. The disciples are measuring nearness physically. Jesus is redefining nearness spiritually. If he remains physically present, his embodied ministry remains localized. If he goes to the Father and sends the Spirit, his presence becomes available to every disciple everywhere.

Exegetical Notes

  • “Sorrow has filled your heart” (John 16:6) names the emotional weight of Jesus’ departure. Jesus does not mock their grief.
  • “I tell you the truth” (John 16:7) signals that what follows will contradict their instincts.
  • “It is to your advantage” translates συμφέρει (sympherei), meaning beneficial, profitable, advantageous.
  • The word is shocking. Jesus does not say, “You will survive my departure.” He says his departure is better for them.
  • The reason is explicit: “If I do not go away, the Advocate will not come to you” (John 16:7).

Ascension Connection

This is the doctrine behind John 20:17.

Mary wants to hold on to the resurrected Jesus. Jesus tells her not to hold on because he has not yet ascended. She is thinking, “I do not want to lose you again.” Jesus is thinking, “I am making it impossible for that to happen.”

The Ascension is not Jesus walking away from intimacy. The Ascension is Jesus taking intimacy global.

Best Teaching Line

Jesus’ physical presence was precious, but it was not the final gift. The final gift was Spirit-mediated union with him.

Application

Many disciples still think like the apostles before the Ascension:

  • “I would be stronger if Jesus were physically here.”
  • “I would grow more if Jesus discipled me directly.”
  • “I would obey better if I could sit across from him.”
  • “I would have more faith if I could hear his voice audibly.”

John 16 confronts that assumption. Jesus says the post-Ascension disciple is not disadvantaged. The post-Ascension disciple has the Spirit.

Preaching Punch

The apostles did not have the best God could offer while Jesus was physically beside them. Jesus says the better gift comes when he goes to the Father and sends the Spirit.

2. The Advocate’s Mission (John 16:8-15)

> “And when he comes, he will convict the world concerning sin and righteousness and judgment… He will glorify me, for he will take what is mine and declare it to you.” – John 16:8, 14****

Main Point: The Spirit does not replace Jesus with a vague spiritual feeling. The Spirit continues Jesus’ work. He exposes the world, guides the disciples, speaks what he receives, reveals what belongs to Christ, and glorifies Jesus.

The Spirit Toward the World (John 16:8-11)

The Spirit will convict/expose the world concerning:

  1. Sin – because they do not believe in Jesus (John 16:9)
  2. Righteousness – because Jesus goes to the Father and is vindicated (John 16:10)
  3. Judgment – because the ruler of this world is judged (John 16:11)

The Spirit Toward Disciples (John 16:12-15)

The Spirit will:

  • Guide them into all truth
  • Speak what he hears
  • Declare what is to come
  • Glorify Jesus
  • Take what belongs to Jesus and declare it to them

Greek Notes

Greek Transliteration Meaning Significance
παράκλητος paraklētos Advocate, Helper, Counselor The Spirit is personal divine assistance, not impersonal energy.
ἐλέγξει elenxei convict, expose, prove wrong The Spirit confronts the world’s false verdict about Jesus.
ὁδηγήσει hodēgēsei he will guide The Spirit guides disciples into truth after Jesus’ departure.
δοξάσει doxasei he will glorify The Spirit’s ministry is Christ-centered.
ἀναγγελεῖ anangelei he will announce/declare The Spirit communicates what belongs to Jesus.

Ascension Connection

In the Ascension lesson, the key claim is that Jesus becomes “infinitely available” through the Spirit.

John 16 clarifies that this does not mean Jesus becomes vague. The Spirit does not give us less Jesus. The Spirit gives us Jesus’ presence, truth, conviction, comfort, and mission in a new mode.

Best Teaching Line

The Spirit is not the consolation prize for losing Jesus. The Spirit is how the ascended Jesus gives himself to his people.

Application

If the Spirit’s mission is to glorify Jesus, then Spirit-filled disciples should become more Jesus-centered, not less.

The evidence of the Spirit is not merely emotion, intensity, or spiritual language. In John 16, the Spirit:

  • Exposes unbelief
  • Vindicates Jesus
  • Guides into truth
  • Glorifies Christ
  • Communicates what belongs to the Son

Restoration Movement Connection

This connects naturally to Acts 2:38. The gift of the Holy Spirit is not ornamental. It is central to post-Ascension discipleship. Baptized disciples do not merely join a movement; they receive the Spirit promised by Jesus and poured out by the exalted Christ.

Preaching Punch

If you are a baptized disciple, you do not have less access to Jesus than the apostles had. You have the Spirit of the ascended Christ dwelling in you.

3. Sorrow Turned to Joy (John 16:16-24)

> “You will be sorrowful, but your sorrow will turn into joy… I will see you again, and your hearts will rejoice, and no one will take your joy from you.” – John 16:20, 22****

Main Point: Jesus does not deny the disciples’ sorrow. He promises its transformation. The cross will feel like loss, but resurrection and Ascension will reveal that the apparent loss was the path to irreversible joy.

Exegetical Notes

  • “A little while” (John 16:16) points to the strange near-future rhythm of death, resurrection, departure, and return in Spirit-mediated presence.
  • Jesus compares their grief to labor pains (John 16:21). The pain is real, but it is not meaningless.
  • “No one will take your joy from you” (John 16:22) means their joy will be anchored in the completed work of Jesus, not fragile circumstances.
  • Prayer in Jesus’ name follows this joy (John 16:23-24). Access to the Father is part of the post-Ascension gift.

Ascension Connection

The Ascension lesson asks:

Wouldn’t it be better to have Jesus physically with us?

John 16 answers:

Not if physical presence would keep you from receiving Spirit-mediated presence, direct access to the Father, and joy no one can take away.

Best Teaching Line

The Ascension does not remove Jesus from the disciples’ joy. It secures the kind of joy that cannot be taken.

Application

Some disciples interpret difficulty as proof of distance from Jesus. John 16 says trouble can coexist with peace, sorrow can become joy, and absence can become deeper presence.

You are not waiting in line for Jesus.

You are not hoping he has time for you.

You are not competing with billions of people for access.

Through the Spirit, the ascended Jesus is present to every disciple.

Preaching Punch

The Ascension means there is no waiting list for Jesus.

4. Scattered Disciples, Victorious Jesus (John 16:25-33)

> “In the world you will have tribulation. But take heart; I have overcome the world.” – John 16:33****

Main Point: Jesus tells the disciples the truth about their weakness and the truth about his victory. They will scatter. He will stand. They will fail. He will overcome. Their peace is not rooted in their consistency but in his completed victory.

Exegetical Notes

  • The disciples think they finally understand (John 16:29-30), but Jesus immediately exposes their coming failure (John 16:31-32).
  • “You will be scattered” echoes shepherd/sheep imagery and anticipates the Passion.
  • “Yet I am not alone” (John 16:32) shows the Son’s communion with the Father even as human companionship collapses.
  • “Tribulation” translates θλῖψις (thlipsis): pressure, distress, affliction.
  • “Take heart” translates θαρσεῖτε (tharseite): be courageous, be encouraged.
  • “I have overcome” translates νενίκηκα (nenikēka), a perfect tense verb: Jesus’ victory is decisive and enduring.

Ascension Connection

The Ascension means the victorious Christ is not merely remembered. He reigns, intercedes, pours out the Spirit, and remains present with his people.

This is why Hebrews 4:14-16 and Hebrews 7:25 matter:

  • We have an ascended high priest.
  • We can approach the throne with confidence.
  • He lives to intercede.

Best Teaching Line

The disciples’ failure is real, but it is not final because Jesus’ victory is greater than their scattering.

Application

John 16 gives believers a sober but hopeful discipleship:

  • Do not be shocked by trouble.
  • Do not be arrogant about your strength.
  • Do not confuse Jesus’ physical absence with abandonment.
  • Do not measure access by emotion.
  • Take heart because the ascended Christ has overcome the world.

Preaching Punch

Jesus does not promise a world without trouble. He promises peace inside a world he has already defeated.

Diagnostic Questions

  1. Do I secretly believe I would be more faithful if Jesus were physically present?
  2. Do I treat the Holy Spirit as less personal or less powerful than Jesus’ physical presence?
  3. Am I living as if Jesus is far away or as if Christ lives in me?
  4. Where has sorrow made me interpret Jesus’ departure as absence rather than advantage?
  5. Do I rely on external spiritual experiences more than Spirit-enabled union with Christ?
  6. Does my view of the Spirit center more on emotion or on glorifying Jesus?
  7. Do I believe the ascended Jesus gives me real access to the Father?
  8. Where do I need to take heart because Jesus has overcome the world?

Gospel Pivot

The gospel is not merely that Jesus came near in the incarnation. The gospel is that the crucified, risen, and ascended Jesus sends the Spirit so that his life becomes present in his people.

Jesus’ physical departure does not leave disciples with memories. It gives them the Advocate. It gives them truth. It gives them access. It gives them joy. It gives them peace. It gives them Christ in them, the hope of glory.

Conclusion Imperatives

  • Do not romanticize pre-Ascension discipleship as if it was better.
  • Do not treat the Spirit as a downgrade from Jesus.
  • Do not confuse physical absence with relational distance.
  • Do not let trouble define Jesus’ victory.
  • Trust the advantage Jesus says you have.
  • Live as a Spirit-indwelt disciple of the ascended Christ.

Sermon Uses from The Ascension

Strong Lines to Reuse

  • Mary was thinking, “I do not want to lose you again.” Jesus was thinking, “I am making it impossible for that to ever happen.”
  • Jesus in physical form was limited to one place at one time.
  • Through the Ascension, Jesus expanded access across the universe.
  • You are not waiting in line for Jesus.
  • The apostles did not have the best God could offer. The Spirit-indwelt kingdom disciple does.
  • The Holy Spirit is not a vague force; he is the presence of the ascended Christ with his people.

Illustration to Reuse

Imagine trying to see Jesus physically today:

  • Waiting list for prayer time
  • Waiting list for marriage counseling
  • Waiting list for Bible study
  • Waiting list for weddings
  • Crowds bringing sick family members
  • Security, hecklers, travel, politics, danger

Then contrast:

Through the Spirit:

  • No line
  • No distance
  • No schedule conflict
  • No competition for attention
  • Full access to Christ
