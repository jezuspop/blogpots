The Son of Man

Follow the title from Ezekiel and Daniel to Jesus’ ministry, suffering, and future vindication.

Study Context

  • "Son of Man" is Jesus’ most frequent self-designation in the Gospels.
  • The phrase has a dual background:
    1. Humanity/mortality (Hebrew ben-adam; especially Ezekiel), and
    2. Exalted heavenly authority (Daniel 7:13–14).
  • Jesus fuses these streams: the Son of Man is the humble sufferer and the enthroned ruler.

Key Themes

  1. Authority Now
    • Forgives sins (Mark 2:10)
    • Lord of Sabbath (Mark 2:28)
    • Has authority to judge (John 5:27)
  2. Suffering Then Glory
    • Must suffer, be rejected, killed, and rise (Mark 8:31; 9:31; 10:33–34)
    • Path to exaltation runs through the cross (cf. Dan 7 + Isa 53 trajectory)
  3. Future Vindication
    • Coming with clouds/power/glory (Mark 13:26; 14:62)
    • End-time judge and gatherer of the elect (Matt 24–25)
  4. Representative Identity
    • True human, faithful Israel, and messianic king in one person
    • Bridges Genesis calling (human vocation) and kingdom consummation

OT Background

  • Daniel 7:13–14 — “one like a son of man” receives dominion, glory, kingdom
  • Psalm 8:4–6 — humanity’s vocation and dignity under God
  • Ezekiel (90+ uses of ‘son of man’ for Ezekiel) — mortal prophet addressed by God
  • Isaiah 52:13–53:12 — suffering servant pattern that informs Jesus’ mission

NT Clusters

  • Authority sayings: Mark 2:10, 2:28
  • Passion predictions: Mark 8:31; 9:31; 10:45
  • Apocalyptic return: Mark 13:26; 14:62; Matt 24:30
  • Johannine lift-up/glory: John 3:13–14; 8:28; 12:23

Passage Study

Introduction

“Son of Man” sounds ordinary to modern ears, but in Scripture it is loaded. In Jesus’ mouth, the term reveals identity gradually: He is fully human, covenantally obedient, destined to suffer, and divinely authorized to reign.

One-sentence summary:

The Son of Man is Jesus’ self-revelation as the suffering representative human who receives everlasting dominion from God.

Key Thought

Jesus uses “Son of Man” not to hide His identity, but to reveal it on God’s timeline—cross first, crown forever.

1 Key Point: Son of Man = Authority on Earth (Present Ministry)

Read the passage: Mark 2:10

Main point

  • Jesus claims divine prerogatives while standing in ordinary human history.
  • The title lets Him reveal messianic authority without collapsing into popular political expectations.
  • He reframes kingdom power around forgiveness, restoration, and obedience.

Application

  • Jesus’ authority is not abstract; it addresses guilt, identity, and discipleship now.

2 Key Point: Son of Man = Suffering Servant (Cross-Shaped Messiah)

Read the passage: Mark 8:31

Main point

  • Jesus repeatedly ties “Son of Man” to rejection, death, and resurrection.
  • He refuses triumphalism: glory is reached through sacrificial obedience.
  • Mark 10:45 gives the mission center: service + ransom.

Application

  • If we follow the Son of Man, we follow a cruciform pattern, not comfort-first religion.

3 Key Point: Son of Man = Cloud-Rider Judge-King (Final Vindication)

Read the passage: Mark 14:62

Main point

  • Direct allusion to Daniel 7: Jesus claims eschatological kingship.
  • The one judged by earthly courts is the one who will judge the world.
  • This is why the claim triggers blasphemy charges in His trial scene.

Application

  • Faithfulness in present suffering is grounded in future vindication under Christ’s reign.

Scholarly Integration & Dissenting Views

View 1: Danielic-Messianic (majority evangelical view) - “Son of Man” primarily echoes Daniel 7 and signals exalted authority. - Strength: Best explains trial texts (Mark 14:62) and cloud-coming language. - Challenge: Must also account for humble/suffering uses.

View 2: Idiomatic Human Reference (Aramaic circumlocution emphasis) - Jesus may sometimes use the phrase as an indirect/self-referential “this man / I.” - Strength: Explains linguistic flexibility in Semitic speech. - Challenge: Underplays clear apocalyptic-royal claims in key texts.

View 3: Corporate Figure (saints of the Most High) with Jesus as representative head - Daniel 7’s “son of man” includes corporate people-of-God dimensions. - Jesus embodies Israel and the faithful remnant as their kingly representative. - Strength: Integrates individual + corporate biblical theology. - Challenge: Can become too diffuse if detached from Jesus’ personal claims.

Assessment: - Strongest reading combines Danielic exaltation + suffering-servant mission + representative humanity in Jesus.

Conclusion

“Son of Man” is not a soft title; it is a strategic, theologically dense self-disclosure. Jesus is the authoritative forgiver, the willing sufferer, and the coming King-Judge.

Call to action: - Submit to His authority, follow His cross-shaped way, and live ready for His return.

Key Language Notes

  • Hebrew: ben-adam = son of man / human one
  • Aramaic (Dan 7): bar enash (“one like a son of man”)
  • Greek NT: ho huios tou anthrōpou (“the Son of Man”)

Restoration Movement Connections

  • Christ’s authority is practical and covenantal: repentance, baptismal faith, transformed discipleship.
  • The church proclaims both the cross (suffering Son of Man) and the kingdom (reigning Son of Man).
  • Calls disciples beyond nominal belief into obedient, mission-shaped life.

Teaching Payoff (short form)

  1. The Son of Man forgives your past.
  2. The Son of Man defines your present path.
  3. The Son of Man secures your future hope.
