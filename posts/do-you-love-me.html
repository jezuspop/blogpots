Topical Sermons November 24, 2026

Do You Love Me?

Jesus calls us friends. Friendship with Him grows out of His self-giving love and is open to people whether or not they ever marry.

  • In the spirit of Valentine's Day: The story of Loch Ard Gorge in Australia
  • In 1878, the Loch Ard wrecked off Victoria, Australia. Of 54 people aboard, only Tom Pearce and Eva Carmichael survived.
  • Tom made it to shore and then went back into the water to rescue Eva.
  • They did not marry. We don’t know enough to speak for either of them about why.
  • Question: Why do we feel disappointed by that ending? Because we can be obsessed with romantic love

Big Idea

Jesus calls us friends. Friendship with Him grows out of His self-giving love and is open to people whether or not they ever marry.

1

Understanding the Four Greek Words for Love

  • These are teaching categories, not rigid definitions for every Greek use. Agapē is a noun and phileō a verb; John 21 compares the verbs agapaō and phileō.
  • Agape (Ἀγάπη) — Unconditional, sacrificial love; delights in giving; keeps loving even when unresponsive (Ephesians 5:25, John 3:16)
  • Storge (Στοργή) — Natural affection, parental love (Romans 12:10)
  • Eros — Romantic, sexual attraction; love of passion; the basic idea can become focused on self-satisfaction
  • Phileo — Affection and fondness, including the love we recognize in friendship

In the church we look to the cross to learn self-giving love. The world around us often markets sex and romance as if they were the whole story.

2

Jesus Asks Peter About Love

"When they had finished eating, Jesus said to Simon Peter, 'Simon son of John, do you love me more than these?' 'Yes, Lord,' he said, 'you know that I love you.' Jesus said, 'Feed my lambs.'"

John 21:15 (NIV)

Two Charcoal Fires

  • Peter warmed himself at a charcoal fire on the night he denied Jesus. At another charcoal fire, the risen Jesus has fish and bread waiting for the disciples. After breakfast come three questions and a charge to care for his sheep. Reading the scenes side by side brings out the restoration. John 18:15–27 · John 21:9–17
  • Jesus asks Peter twice, "Do you agape me?"
  • But the third time, He asks, "Do you phileo me?"
  • Peter uses phileō each time. John uses both verbs for the Father’s love for the Son (John 3:35; 5:20), so the change may be stylistic rather than a ranking of loves. The clearer emphasis is Jesus asking Peter to care for His sheep.
  • My reading and application: Jesus’ questions make me think about what it means to love Him as a friend.
    • Jesus takes the initiative in love and invites us to respond. Giving and receiving both belong in this friendship.
    • Friendship need not revolve around exclusivity; it can make room for others.
    • It doesn't require a dating relationship or marriage
  • Phileo love is the love everyone is called by Jesus to share
3

Called to Be Friends of Jesus

"My command is this: Love each other as I have loved you. Greater love has no one than this: to lay down one's life for one's friends. You are my friends if you do what I command. I no longer call you servants, because a servant does not know his master's business. Instead, I have called you friends ..."

John 15:12–15 (NIV, excerpt)
  • Jesus calls us friends, not servants
  • A servant doesn't know his master's business — but Jesus has made everything known to us
  • This is the love Jesus challenges Peter to have after the crucifixion
  • Question: What is your understanding of love?
    • Are you caught up like the world with Eros — looking for relationships, concerned only about romance?
    • Are you striving to love Jesus with phileo love?

Conclusion

  • Summary: The Bible gives us a richer vocabulary for love than the world offers — and Jesus calls us to the kind of friendship that gives itself for others
  • Call to Action: Let friendship with Jesus and with others shape how you love.
  • Closing: As disciples, we must be careful not to follow the patterns of this world (Romans 12:2). Let's get our understanding of love from the Bible, not the world.
