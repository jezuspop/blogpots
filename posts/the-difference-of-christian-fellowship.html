Topical Sermons November 24, 2026

The Difference of Christian Fellowship

Christian fellowship is like nothing else; meaningful relationships, people who challenge and love you, opportunity to serve, and having fun without…

  • Theme this year: Deeply Rooted — this month's focus is Fellowship
  • Grateful for the fellowship in the church, especially after our trip to California
  • We are all important to fellowship — but no part is important on its own

Big Idea

Christian fellowship is like nothing else — meaningful relationships, people who challenge and love you, opportunity to serve, and having fun without next-day guilt. Invest in your church family.

1

Every Part Is Indispensable

Paul’s picture of the church as a body

The eye cannot say to the hand, 'I don't need you!' And the head cannot say to the feet, 'I don't need you!' On the contrary, those parts of the body that seem to be weaker are indispensable... If one part suffers, every part suffers with it; if one part is honored, every part rejoices with it.

1 Corinthians 12:21–22, 26 (NIV)
  • The unpresentable parts of the body are just as important — sometimes vital
  • One part hurts, the rest hurt and help with healing
  • If we succeed, we succeed together — we're as strong as our weakest link
  • Personal: I'm selfish by nature and always tempted not to ask for help. But I felt the impact of fellowship on my trip to California.
2

The Unsung Heroes of Rome

Paul commends Phoebe to the church at Rome

I commend to you our sister Phoebe, a deacon of the church in Cenchreae... for she has been the benefactor of many people, including me.

Romans 16:1–2 (NIV)

Historical Context: Fellowship in People’s Homes

  • Paul greets a church that meets in Priscilla and Aquila’s home. Acts also describes the couple taking Apollos aside and explaining God’s way more fully. Their example gives us a picture of both a gathered church and a personal conversation that helps someone grow. Romans 16:3–5 · Acts 18:24–26
  • Phoebe likely carried Paul’s letter. Romans 16:1–2 commends her and asks the church to receive and help her; it doesn’t name her as the courier. Think of our service team members who help get the message where it needs to go.
  • Priscilla and Aquila (v. 3-4) — risked their lives for Paul and explained the way of God more fully to Apollos (Acts 18:24–26). We need strong married couples.
  • Epenetus (v. 5) — the first convert to Christ in the province of Asia. New converts add to the fellowship.
  • Mary (v. 6) — worked very hard. You may not lead anything but you can still have impact.
  • Andronicus and Junia (v. 7) — in Christ before Paul. Older Christians show us it's worth the fight.
  • Apelles (v. 10) — whose fidelity to Christ had stood the test. Disciples who endure hardship are pillars.
  • Herodion (v. 11) — Paul's fellow Jew. Christians of similar background are encouraging.
  • Tryphena, Tryphosa, and Persis (v. 12) — faithful sisters whose work Paul names and honors.
  • Rufus's mother (v. 13) — who was like a mother to Paul. Mothers in Christ add value.
3

The Value We Bring

  • Service team members — without them, the message doesn't get delivered
  • Strong marriages — showing what committed, giving marriages look like
  • New converts — reminding us people still need to be saved
  • Hard workers — God needs more workers (Luke 10:2)
  • Older Christians — it would be weird if the church were all young people
  • Tested disciples — their endurance gives the rest of us an example of faithfulness
  • Similar backgrounds — seeing them make it gives hope that you can too
  • Faithful sisters — fighters, spiritual, fruitful, sacrificial
  • Spiritual mothers — who care for others as their own

Conclusion

  • Summary: There is great value in Christian fellowship — if you're looking for meaningful relationships, challenge, opportunity to serve, and guilt-free fun, you've come to the right place
  • Call to Action: Invest more into your church community. Build new relationships with members you don't know well. Strengthen old ones. Share this gift with the world around you.
  • Closing: Christian fellowship is like nothing else.
