Ephesians 2:1–10: Dead to Alive by Grace

Trace the passage from spiritual death to new life, and see how grace and good works fit together.

Passage Study

Read the passage: Ephesians 2:1–10

Ephesians 2:10 NLT

Created for Good Works

Read Ephesians 2:10

Introduction

Paragraph movement: 1) What we were: dead, enslaved, condemned (vv.1–3) 2) What God did: made alive, raised, seated with Christ (vv.4–6) 3) Why God did it: display immeasurable grace (v.7) 4) How salvation works: grace through faith, not self-achievement (vv.8–9) 5) Who we are: his masterpiece (v.10)

One-sentence summary: Ephesians 2:1–10 declares that salvation is entirely God-initiated grace in Christ that creates a new obedient people.

Key Thought

Grace is not only the cause of our rescue; it is also the architect of our new way of life.

1 Key Point: Humanity’s Condition Is Spiritual Death, Not Mere Weakness (vv.1–3)

Read the passage: Ephesians 2:1–2

Main point

  • Paul describes pre-conversion life as death, not moral inconvenience.
  • Threefold bondage appears:
    • World (age-pattern rebellion)
    • Devil ("prince of the power of the air")
    • Flesh (disordered desires)
  • Arnold (ZECNT): Spiritual powers language is concrete in Paul’s worldview, not decorative metaphor.
  • Hoehner: "children of wrath" indicates real covenant-judicial exposure, not merely emotional consequence.
  • Greek note:
    • νεκρούς (nekrous) = dead (spiritually incapacitated apart from divine action).

Application

  • The gospel must be preached as resurrection power, not self-improvement strategy.
  • Pastoral caution: minimizing sin minimizes grace.

2 Key Point: “But God” Is the Turning Point of the Gospel (vv.4–7)

Read the passage: Ephesians 2:4

Main point

  • Salvation begins in God’s character: mercy + love + grace.
  • Verbs are divine-action heavy: made alive, raised, seated with Christ.
  • O’Brien (Pillar): Union-with-Christ language grounds both present identity and future hope.
  • F.F. Bruce: v.7 frames redemption as cosmic testimony to grace across ages.
  • Theological center: God does not meet us halfway; He rescues the dead.

Application

  • Assurance is anchored in God’s action, not spiritual mood.
  • Worship response: gratitude over boasting.

3 Key Point: Saved by Grace Through Faith—Not by Works (vv.8–9)

Read the passage: Ephesians 2:8–9

Main point

  • Paul excludes all salvation boasting while preserving real faith response.
  • Thielman (BECNT): Paul’s stress is anti-boast and anti-self-salvation, not anti-obedience.
  • Schreiner: Faith is receptive trust, never meritorious contribution.
  • Translation nuance:
    • ESV/NIV/NLT all preserve grace-as-source, faith-as-means pattern.

Dissenting views (required)

  • View 1 (Monergistic): Deadness implies total inability; grace effectually creates saving response.
    • Strength: strong fit with vv.1–5 divine initiative.
    • Challenge: must articulate how accountability remains meaningful.
  • View 2 (Synergistic): Grace initiates and enables; humans genuinely respond in faith.
    • Strength: preserves universal summons and response language.
    • Challenge: must avoid making faith function as a meritorious work.

Assessment: High confidence in grace as source and boasting excluded; moderate-confidence debate on grace/faith mechanics among orthodox interpreters.

Application

  • Reject both legalism (earning) and passivity (no transformation).
  • Gospel invitation: receive, don’t perform.

4 Key Point: Grace Creates a Walking People (v.10)

Read the passage: Ephesians 2:10

Main point

  • Believers are God’s ποίημα (poiēma) — crafted work/product.
  • Good works are not the root of salvation but the designed path of the saved.
  • Arnold + O’Brien: v.10 balances vv.8–9 by showing grace’s ethical goal.
  • CoC resonance (best case): obedience is covenant fruit of grace, not competitor to grace.

Application

  • Ask not only “Was I saved?” but “Am I walking in what God prepared?”
  • Discipleship metric: trajectory of obedient formation.

Conclusion

Ephesians 2:1–10 rebukes human boasting, magnifies divine mercy, and commissions grace-saved people into grace-shaped good works. Paul’s gospel is both courtroom acquittal and new-creation calling.

Call to action: Remember what you were, rejoice in what God did, and walk intentionally in what God prepared.

Closing image: The God who raised you from death also mapped your next faithful steps.

Key Greek Words

  • νεκρός (nekros) — dead; spiritual incapacity apart from God.
  • χάρις (charis) — grace; unearned divine favor and saving action.
  • πίστις (pistis) — faith; trustful reception, covenant allegiance.
  • ποίημα (poiēma) — workmanship; crafted product of divine artistry.
  • περιπατέω (peripateō) — walk; habitual life-pattern.

  • Harmonizes with Acts pattern: genuine response to gospel yields transformed life.
