Topical Sermons November 24, 2026

When People Don’t Get It — The Power of Perception

An interactive discussion on how expectations shape what we see and how to help someone see Scripture with fresh eyes.

Interactive Midweek Lesson (Discussion-Integrated) v2

Target Length: 45 minutes (can compress to 35 with optional cuts marked below)

Big Idea

People don’t miss truth because Scripture is unclear; they miss truth because their lens is misaligned. In ministry, our role is not just to present verses—it’s to help people see through the right lens.

One of the most frustrating moments in ministry is studying with someone and thinking:
“Why don’t they see this? It’s right there in the text.”

You explain it. You read it together. You ask good questions.
And still—they don’t get it.

But what if the issue is not intelligence?
What if the issue is perception?

Discussion Pause #1

  • Think of a time you misunderstood a Bible passage at first. What changed?
  • Have you ever had a moment where Scripture was always there, but you suddenly saw it differently?

Transition line: Let’s watch how Jesus dealt with this in real time.

1

Exposition

The barrier is often perception, not information

Texts: Mark 6:52; Mark 8:17–18; Mark 8:22–26

In Mark, this is a repeated pattern:

  • People witness power but miss meaning.
  • They see miracles but don’t grasp identity.

Mark snapshots

Mark 6:52
— disciples saw the feeding, but their hearts were hardened.

Mark 8:17–18
— Jesus asks, “Do you still not see or understand?... having eyes do you not see?”

Mark 8:22–26
— blind man healed in two stages: first partial sight, then clarity.

This two-stage healing acts like a mirror of the disciples’ spiritual journey. They are not totally blind—but not yet clear.

Discussion Pause #2

  • Why do you think Jesus sometimes asks questions instead of just giving answers?
  • In spiritual growth, where do you see “partial vision” in your own life right now?

Ministry Insight:
People can be sincere, teachable, and still see only partially. That should shape our patience.

2

Exposition

Wrong expectations can hide obvious truth

Texts: Mark 8:31; Mark 9:11–12; Isaiah 53; Psalm 22; Daniel 9:26

Jesus repeatedly tells the disciples the Son of Man must suffer, be rejected, and be killed (Mark 8:31).
They struggle because their framework is off:

  • Their expectation: Messiah = conquering king only
  • Jesus’ revelation: Messiah = conquering king and suffering servant

In Mark 9:11–12, the disciples ask why Elijah must come first. Jesus answers, then raises a second question about the Son of Man’s suffering and rejection.

The issue is not absent data.
The issue is selective reading through a preferred lens.

Discussion Pause #3

  • What kinds of expectations can keep people from accepting plain biblical teaching?
  • Which is harder for you personally: learning new truth, or unlearning old assumptions?

Ministry Insight:
Spiritual blindness often appears as selective emphasis—holding tightly to texts that comfort us while minimizing texts that confront us.

3

Exposition

Transformation happens when the lens is addressed

Texts: Luke 24:17, 24:21, 24:27; John 4:20–24; Acts 17:2–3

Jesus shows a repeatable discipleship pattern.

The pattern

  1. Draw out perspective — “What are you discussing?” (Luke 24:17)
  2. Expose assumptions — “We had hoped...” (Luke 24:21)
  3. Reframe through Scripture — Moses and Prophets rightly interpreted (Luke 24:27)

Same movement in John 4:

  • Start with her framework (place of worship)
  • Reframe to true worship (spirit and truth)

Paul echoes this pattern in Acts 17:2–3: reasoning from Scripture that Messiah had to suffer and rise.

Discussion Pause #4

  • Which step do we skip most in our Bible studies: listening, clarifying, or reframing?
  • What might change if we spent more time understanding people before correcting them?

Ministry Insight:
Breakthrough often comes when people are helped to compare their assumptions with the full witness of Scripture.

Application

For an intimate ministry setting

When someone “doesn’t get it,” avoid labeling them too quickly.
Don’t assume rebellion where there may be confusion, fear, or inherited framing.

Lens-shaping factors to watch for

  • Religious tradition
  • Cultural assumptions
  • Fear of consequences
  • Pride / self-protection
  • Personal pain
  • Distorted image of God

Practical discipleship flow (walk-around friendly)

  1. Understand perception — ask, listen, observe tone/emotion
  2. Clarify framework — reflect assumptions back with kindness
  3. Reveal truth — place Scripture and assumptions side-by-side
  4. Invite response — “What do you think Jesus is asking you to do with this?”

Discussion Pause #5

  • Who is one person in your life you need more patience with in Bible study?
  • What is one question you can ask this week that helps reveal their lens?

Walk Away + Close

People don’t only need more verses—they need a clearer lens.
Our calling is to patiently shepherd perception until people can see what God has already said.

Closing line:
“Sometimes the breakthrough in Bible study is not new information—it’s new sight.”

Optional Closing Prayer

“Father, give us eyes to see and ears to hear. Help us handle Your word with humility and patience. Teach us to love people enough to listen before we correct, and to trust Your Spirit to open hearts in Your timing. In Jesus’ name, amen.”

Optional 35-Minute Compression Plan

If you need to shorten in real time:

  • Trim each discussion pause by 1 minute
  • Keep one key cross-reference per point
  • Reduce Application section to the 4-step flow only
  • Keep full Walk Away close
