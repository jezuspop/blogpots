Biblical Definitions of Key Terms

Trace key Bible words in their passages rather than reducing them to quick definitions out of context.

Method: theological descriptions are not exhaustive dictionary entries

Read each passage in its argument before treating it as a definition. These texts explain dimensions of faith, love, worship, and identity; no single sentence necessarily exhausts the semantic range of a Hebrew or Greek word.

Important contextual checks

  • Hebrews 11:1: Read from 10:35-39 through the examples in chapter 11. This is faith amid waiting and perseverance, not a stand-alone philosophical definition detached from hope and obedience.
  • John 8:31: Jesus identifies abiding as the mark of genuine discipleship. This supports a theology of persevering belief, but “abide” is not the dictionary meaning of pisteuō.
  • 1 Corinthians 13: The concrete description of love addresses the use of gifts in a divided body (chapters 12-14), not only romance or private virtue.
  • John 4:23-24: Keep the Samaritan-Jewish worship dispute, the coming hour, and Jesus’ revelation in view. “Spirit and truth” cannot simply be reduced to sincerity plus correct ritual form; interpreters discuss the role of God’s Spirit and worship enabled by Jesus.
  • Romans 2:28-29: Paul challenges confidence in outward status without covenant faithfulness. Interpret this within Romans 2-3 and 9-11, not as permission to erase Jewish ethnicity or despise Jewish people.
  • Love and sin vocabulary: The agapaō/agapē word family does not inherently mean divine, unconditional love in every use (compare loving darkness in John 3:19). “Missing the mark” must not replace the contextual moral meaning of sin through an etymological shortcut.
  • Neighbor and greatness: Luke 10 moves from classifying a neighbor to becoming a merciful neighbor. Mark 10’s summons to service applies to the whole disciple community, not only men or officeholders.

Study procedure

  1. Read the paragraph and identify the speaker, audience, and issue.
  2. Paraphrase what this text affirms, without claiming it says everything about the term.
  3. Compare other usages and passages before making a broad theological definition.
  4. Separate the passage’s teaching from your application.

Big Idea

The Bible often defines its own major terms not by modern dictionary-style entries, but by direct statements, explanatory contrasts, covenant context, and repeated usage inside key passages.

Driving Question

Where does Scripture most clearly define its own theological, moral, and discipleship terms, and how can those texts keep us from using biblical language in vague or unbiblical ways?

Topic map

1. Scripture often defines terms from within the text itself

  • Some texts are close to explicit dictionary definitions.
  • Others define by explanation, contrast, or immediate application.
  • That means careful Bible study should ask, “How does this passage itself explain the term?”

2. The clearest definitions usually appear in doctrinally loaded passages

  • Jesus often defines response terms like belief, freedom, worship, and discipleship.
  • Paul often defines covenant and salvation terms like gospel, church, circumcision, and sanctification.
  • John often defines moral and relational terms like sin, love, and eternal life.
  • James often defines lived religion, wisdom, and worldliness in concrete ways.

3. Many church arguments become clearer when the definitional texts are put first

  • What is faith?
  • What is belief?
  • What is repentance?
  • What is love?
  • What is worship?
  • What is the church?
  • What is friendship with the world?
  • What is true greatness?

4. A good study method is to build outward from the clearest defining texts

  • Start with the text that most explicitly explains the term.
  • Then expand to broader biblical theology.
  • Do not let later tradition, loose church language, or modern sentiment override the definitional force of the passage.

Exegetical Anchors

Faith, belief, and response

  • Hebrews 11:1-2 – faith explained
  • John 8:31-32 – belief / discipleship defined through abiding
  • John 17:3 – eternal life defined relationally
  • John 3:3-5 – new birth explained
  • 1 Corinthians 15:1-4 – gospel summarized in core content

Sin, repentance, and moral clarity

  • 1 John 3:4 – sin defined as lawlessness
  • 2 Corinthians 7:10 – godly sorrow contrasted with worldly sorrow
  • James 1:14-15 – temptation’s progression explained
  • Mark 7:20-23 – defilement traced to the heart
  • Colossians 3:5 – greed identified as idolatry

Love, worship, and practical religion

  • 1 Corinthians 13:4-8 – love described concretely
  • 1 John 5:3 – love for God defined by obedience
  • John 4:23-24 – true worshipers defined
  • James 1:27 – pure religion defined
  • Isaiah 58:3-7 – false fasting contrasted with true fasting

Identity, church, and kingdom life

  • Ephesians 1:22-23 – the church defined as Christ’s body
  • Philippians 3:3 – true circumcision defined
  • Romans 2:28-29 – true Jew explained inwardly
  • Mark 10:42-45 – greatness redefined through servanthood
  • Luke 10:29-37 – neighbor defined by mercy, not proximity

Background Notes

A caution on “definition”

  • Not every biblical definition sounds like a modern dictionary.
  • Scripture regularly defines through:
    • direct statements: “faith is…”
    • contrasts: “not this, but this”
    • explanatory clauses: “by this we know…”
    • narrative clarification: “who was the neighbor?”
  • So some texts below are strict definitional texts, while others are functional definitional texts.

Another caution on Bible versions

  • Since translations differ, this note focuses on the definitional force of the passage rather than one exact English wording.
  • In other words, the goal is not to defend one translation choice, but to show where the text itself most clearly explains the concept.

Why this matters for ministry

  • Many Bible studies become vague because people use terms like faith, belief, love, repentance, worship, or church without asking what the Bible means by them.
  • Definitional texts help protect preaching from emotionalism, imprecision, and theological drift.

Key Terms and Definitional Texts

Term Definitional Text Biblical Definition / Definitional Force Why it matters
Faith Hebrews 11:1-2 Faith is presented as confident assurance regarding what is hoped for and conviction regarding what is not yet seen. Keeps faith from being reduced to mere optimism or wishful thinking.
Belief / true believing John 8:31-32 Jesus identifies continuing in his word as the mark of genuine discipleship, testing the depth of professed belief. Strong text against reducing belief to bare mental agreement.
Disciple John 8:31 A true disciple is one who remains in Jesus’ teaching. Shows discipleship is perseverance in truth, not momentary interest.
Freedom John 8:32-36 Freedom is not self-definition; it is liberation through the truth and through the Son. Clarifies Christian freedom as deliverance from slavery to sin.
Eternal life John 17:3 Eternal life is defined relationally: knowing the only true God and Jesus Christ whom he sent. Guards against treating eternal life as mere duration without relationship.
New birth / born again John 3:3-5 New birth is tied to entering the kingdom through birth from above / of water and Spirit. Keeps the phrase from becoming a vague emotional slogan.
Gospel 1 Corinthians 15:1-4 Paul gives the core content: Christ died for sins, was buried, and was raised according to the Scriptures. A foundational text for defining gospel content clearly.
Sin 1 John 3:4 Sin is lawlessness; it is not merely personal brokenness but rebellion against God’s order. Keeps sin from being softened into mere dysfunction.
Temptation’s process James 1:14-15 Temptation proceeds from one’s own desire, which conceives sin, and sin matures into death. Helpful for counseling and teaching the anatomy of sin.
Defilement Mark 7:20-23 Defilement comes from within the heart, then expresses itself outwardly. Refocuses purity from externals to inner corruption.
Repentance by contrast 2 Corinthians 7:10 Godly sorrow produces repentance leading to salvation; worldly sorrow produces death. Clarifies that repentance is more than regret or shame.
Repentance by fruit Matthew 3:8 Repentance is shown by fruit fitting / worthy of repentance. A strong functional definition: repentance must become visible.
Love 1 Corinthians 13:4-8 Love is defined behaviorally: patient, kind, not arrogant, not self-seeking, enduring, etc. One of the clearest practical definitions of love in Scripture.
Love for God 1 John 5:3 Love for God is defined as keeping his commands. Essential corrective against purely emotional definitions of love for God.
What love is 1 John 4:10 Love is defined Christologically: not our prior love for God, but God’s initiating love in sending his Son. Roots love in the gospel rather than sentiment.
Greater love John 15:13 Greater love is defined sacrificially: laying down one’s life for friends. Powerful for men, brotherhood, and Christ-shaped sacrifice.
True worshipers John 4:23-24 True worship is defined in relation to spirit and truth, not merely place or ritual setting. Critical for defining worship biblically instead of stylistically.
Pure religion James 1:27 Pure religion is defined by compassionate care for the vulnerable and personal holiness from the world. Helps expose merely ceremonial religion.
True fasting Isaiah 58:3-7 True fasting is defined not by empty self-denial, but by justice, mercy, release, and generosity. Strong prophetic corrective for hollow religious performance.
Wisdom from above James 3:13-17 True wisdom is pure, peaceable, gentle, open to reason, full of mercy, impartial, and sincere. Gives a behavioral definition of wisdom rather than merely intellectual sharpness.
Worldly wisdom James 3:14-16 False wisdom is marked by jealousy, selfish ambition, disorder, and evil practice. Useful when distinguishing godly leadership from fleshly ambition.
Neighbor Luke 10:29-37 Jesus defines neighbor not by tribal nearness, but by the one who shows mercy. Crucial for love-of-neighbor teaching.
Greatness Mark 10:42-45 Greatness is redefined through servanthood; firstness is redefined through slavery to all. A major text for Christ-shaped discipleship and leadership across the whole church.
Ministry / servant leadership Mark 10:43-45 Ministry is modeled by the Son of Man, who came not to be served but to serve and give his life. Gives the church a Christ-defined leadership model.
Church Ephesians 1:22-23 The church is defined as Christ’s body, the fullness of him who fills all in all. Gives ecclesiology its Christ-centered identity.
Church in bodily terms Colossians 1:18 Christ is head of the body, the church. Reinforces that church is not merely a building or meeting.
True circumcision Philippians 3:3 True circumcision is defined by worship by the Spirit, glorying in Christ, and rejecting fleshly confidence. Important for covenant identity and anti-legalism.
True Jew Romans 2:28-29 A true Jew is defined inwardly, with heart-circumcision by the Spirit rather than mere outward mark. Helps explain inward covenant identity.
Friendship with the world James 4:4 Friendship with the world is defined as hostility toward God. A critical text on divided allegiance.
Worldliness 1 John 2:15-17 Worldliness is summarized in desires of the flesh, desires of the eyes, and pride of life. Gives a compact definition of worldly orientation.
Idolatry Colossians 3:5 Greed / covetousness is explicitly identified as idolatry. Shows idolatry is not limited to statues and pagan ritual.
Murder in heart form 1 John 3:15 Hatred is treated as murder in moral essence. Reveals how Scripture internalizes the commandment.
Adultery in heart form Matthew 5:27-28 Lustful looking is treated as adultery in the heart. Another major text for internalizing righteousness.
Sanctification 1 Thessalonians 4:3-7 God’s will for sanctification is explained concretely in holiness, especially sexual purity and honor. Keeps sanctification from becoming abstract church language.
Hope Romans 8:24-25 Hope is tied to what is not yet seen and therefore involves waiting with endurance. Useful in defining Christian hope beyond positive thinking.
Conversion / new creation 2 Corinthians 5:17 In Christ, the old has passed and new creation has come. Strong identity-definition text for conversion language.
Blessedness / forgiveness Romans 4:6-8 Blessedness is tied to forgiven sin and non-imputation of guilt. Helpful for grace, justification, and assurance.

Terms Especially Worth Memorizing

If you want the shortest “go-to” definitional texts, start here:

  1. Faith – Hebrews 11:1-2
  2. Belief / disciple – John 8:31-32
  3. Eternal life – John 17:3
  4. Sin – 1 John 3:4
  5. Love for God – 1 John 5:3
  6. Love – 1 Corinthians 13:4-8
  7. Gospel – 1 Corinthians 15:1-4
  8. Pure religion – James 1:27
  9. Worship – John 4:23-24
  10. Wisdom from above – James 3:13-17
  11. Greatness – Mark 10:42-45
  12. Church – Ephesians 1:22-23

Teaching applications (not historical claims)

  • If the Bible defines the term, the preacher does not get to redefine it sentimentally.
  • Many weak Bible studies happen because the room uses church words without biblical content.
  • Scripture is often stricter, richer, and more practical than our slogans.
  • The safest way to teach a biblical term is to start where the Bible itself explains it most clearly.

Diagnostic Questions

  1. Am I using biblical terms emotionally or traditionally without checking how Scripture defines them?
  2. When I say “faith,” “belief,” “repentance,” or “love,” do I mean what the text means?
  3. Have I let church culture redefine words that Scripture already explained?
  4. Do my studies start with definitional passages, or with assumptions?
  5. Which terms in my teaching ministry most need to be re-anchored in Scripture?

Christian theological synthesis

One of the clearest things this kind of study reveals is that the Bible’s key words are almost always cross-shaped, covenantal, and relational. Faith is not vague spirituality. Belief is not casual agreement. Love is not mere sentiment. Worship is not performance. The gospel is not advice.

When Scripture defines its own language, it keeps pulling us back to Jesus: knowing him, obeying him, trusting him, loving as he loved, being formed into his body, and receiving the salvation he accomplished. Definitional clarity is not mere semantics. It protects the gospel itself.

Term Transliteration Why it matters here
πίστις pistis Faith; often richer than bare assent, carrying trust, fidelity, and reliance
πιστεύω pisteuō Believe; important in John’s Gospel where believing is tied to abiding and ongoing response
ζωή αἰώνιος zōē aiōnios Eternal life; more than duration, bound up with knowing God and Christ
ἁμαρτία hamartia Sin; moral wrongdoing in context, not a meaning established by the English image “missing the mark”
ἀγάπη agapē Love; defined by Christ’s action and covenantal obedience, not just emotion
εὐαγγέλιον euangelion Gospel; the good news centered on Christ’s death and resurrection
ἐκκλησία ekklēsia Church / assembly; vital for a biblical definition of the people of God
σοφία sophia Wisdom; James especially contrasts heavenly and earthly forms
